Quick Tips From the Trenches

Exclusive insights — only in the newsletter

The 3-Bucket Sort

Before you dial a fresh batch of aged leads, sort them into three buckets: under 90 days old, 90-365 days, and 1+ year. Call the youngest bucket first — contact rates hold up better closer to the original inquiry — but don't skip the older buckets, just batch them for a text-first outreach instead of cold calls. Matching your channel to the age of the lead, not just calling everyone the same way, is the single biggest efficiency gain most agents never make.

The 'Still Looking?' Text Reset

For leads that are 6+ months old and have gone cold on calls, send a single text: "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Company] — you looked into [product] a while back. Still on your radar, or should I close your file?" That last phrase does the work — it gives people permission to say no, which paradoxically makes them more likely to respond either way. Track response rate separately from your call-first sequences; it'll usually outperform a cold voicemail on leads this old.

The Two-Minute Rule for Aged Voicemails

If you leave a voicemail on an aged lead, follow it within two minutes with a text that mirrors the voicemail's first line. People check texts faster than voicemails, and the redundancy reinforces that a real person just called — not a robocall. This one habit alone can lift callback rates on aged data because it solves the 'unknown number, didn't answer' trust gap.

Industry Insight

The one-to-one consent rule is gone — but put January 2027 on your calendar

In January 2025 the 11th Circuit vacated the FCC's 2024 one-to-one consent rule (Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC), and the FCC formally repealed it in August 2025. So it isn't just paused — it's off the books. That's real relief if you buy shared or aged leads. What did not go away: the underlying TCPA, DNC obligations, and state mini-TCPAs in Florida, Oklahoma, Washington, Maryland and Texas, which several of you sell into. And the FCC's revocation rule — one opt-out has to stop all your messaging to that consumer — was delayed, not cancelled: it takes effect January 2027. If your opt-outs live in one system and your dialer lives in another, that's the gap to close this year, not next. The hygiene stack (DNC scrub, litigator scrub, line-type validation) is what carries you through either way.
