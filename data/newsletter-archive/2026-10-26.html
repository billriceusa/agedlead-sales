Quick Tips From the Trenches

Exclusive insights — only in the newsletter

The 7-day cadence, hour by hour

Treat this as a framework and adjust it to your market. Day 1: dial within the first hour of loading the list, then try a second window around 4:30-5:30 PM local. Day 2: a different daypart, such as 8:30-9:30 AM. Day 3: a short text at midday, only to numbers you have consent to text. Day 4: call in the 12:15-1:00 PM window. Day 5: voicemail plus a one-line follow-up. Day 6: rest the lead. Day 7: a closing call around 6:00 PM. Rotating the time of day matters because it catches people in different parts of their routine, and it stays safely inside the federal 8 AM-9 PM local calling window.

What to change on touch four

If three touches got nothing, repeating the same ask a fourth time will get the same result. Change three things: the channel, the time of day, and the size of the ask. Drop 'Can we set a meeting?' for a one-question choice: 'Hi [Name], it's [You]. I've tried you a few times and don't want to be a pest. Is it better if I close your file, or is [topic] still worth ten minutes?' People who ignore a pitch often answer a question that lets them say 'not now' without friction, and either answer tells you what to do with the lead.

The re-engagement text that doesn't read as spam

Keep it short, human, and answerable in two words: 'Hi Maria, it's Dana with [Company]. You asked about [topic] a while back. Still looking, or should I close your file? Reply STOP to opt out.' Leave out links, all-caps, and urgency language in the first message, and give the lead one easy question to answer. Only text numbers you have the right to text, and honor every STOP immediately. If you're unsure where your lead data stands on consent, confirm with your compliance counsel before you send.

Industry Insight

Speed to first contact still decides who gets the conversation

A widely cited Harvard Business Review study, 'The Short Life of Online Sales Leads' (Oldroyd, McElheran, and Elkington, 2011), found that firms contacting web leads within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify them as firms that waited even an hour longer. That research looked at fresh web leads, not aged ones, but the principle carries over. A batch you work the day it lands gets a real cadence, while a batch that sits becomes a backlog. That's one more reason a steady, right-sized restock beats a giant occasional buy you can't work in time.
