Quick Tips From the Trenches

Exclusive insights — only in the newsletter

The 3-Touch Rule for Day One

When a batch of aged leads lands, don't just dial once and move on. Call, then follow with a text 20 minutes later ('Tried you a sec ago, worth 5 minutes to see if [rate/plan] still makes sense for you?'), then a voicemail if the second call also misses. Three touches in the first hour triples your contact rate versus a single dial-and-forget approach, and it costs you nothing but discipline.

Age-Tier Your Dial List

Don't work a 6-month-old lead the same way you work a 6-week-old one. Sort your list into tiers (0-60 days, 61-180 days, 180+ days) and open each tier with a different frame — recent leads get a direct pitch, older leads get a 'checking back in, rates/options have changed' angle. It takes ten minutes in a spreadsheet and it changes your entire opener strategy.

The 'Still Looking?' Voicemail

For leads you can't reach after 3+ attempts, leave this exact voicemail: 'Hey [Name], this is [You] with [Company] — you'd looked into [product/service] a while back and I wanted to see if that's still something on your radar or if life got in the way. No pressure either way, just text me back YES or NO and I'll take it from there.' The binary ask gets replies from people who'd never call you back on their own.

Industry Insight

The Regulatory Ground Just Shifted — Again

The FCC's proposed 'one-to-one consent' rule — which would have made it far harder to legally work purchased leads — was vacated by the 11th Circuit Court of Appeals in January 2025 and hasn't been revived. That means purchased aged leads remain a legal channel, but the FCC's separate revocation-of-consent rule (effective April 2025) now requires you to honor opt-out requests within 10 business days regardless of how they're communicated. Translation: legal to call, but your suppression list discipline matters more than ever.
