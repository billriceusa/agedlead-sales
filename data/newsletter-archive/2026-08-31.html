Work Aged Leads

Weekly strategies for sales professionals — by Bill Rice

Twenty-five years in this business taught me one thing about the week before Labor Day: it's the most wasted week in sales. Half your competitors are already mentally on the boat. Call volume drops, dials get half-hearted, and everyone tells themselves they'll 'really get after it' after the holiday.

Here's what I've watched happen every single year since the Quicken Loans days: the agents who use this quiet week to clean their data, reactivate cold leads, and load up on aged inventory are the ones cashing checks in October while everyone else is still trying to remember their script. Slow weeks don't kill pipelines. Neglected ones do.

This week's newsletter is built around that idea — tightening up your compliance posture, working the leads sitting stale in your CRM, and getting your fall pipeline stacked before the post-Labor Day rush makes everything more expensive and more competitive.

Quick Tips From the Trenches

Exclusive insights — only in the newsletter

The 'Tuesday After' Rule

Don't waste dials this Friday through Monday chasing people who are grilling burgers or driving to the lake. Instead, spend that time building your call list, refreshing scripts, and scrubbing data — then hit the phones hard the Tuesday after Labor Day when everyone's back at their desk and answering unknown numbers again. Your competitors who took the whole week off will be starting cold; you'll already be warmed up.

The Summer Reactivation Text

For aged leads that went quiet over the summer, send this exact text before you call: "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Company] — hope you had a good summer. Following up on the [insurance/mortgage/etc.] info you looked into a while back. Still worth a quick conversation?" This works because it acknowledges the time gap instead of pretending it didn't happen, which builds trust instantly with skeptical aged-lead prospects.

The 3-Bucket September Sort

Before you start dialing post-holiday, sort your aged leads into three buckets: Bucket 1 (contacted but no answer, dial again immediately), Bucket 2 (had a conversation but went cold, needs a fresh angle or new offer), Bucket 3 (never reached, treat as brand new). Working these buckets in that order — instead of top-to-bottom on an old spreadsheet — typically gets you to your best conversations fastest.

Industry Insight

The One-to-One Consent Rule Is Dead — For Now

In January 2025, the 11th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals vacated the FCC's one-to-one consent rule for lead generators (Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC), which means aggregated consent language on lead forms remains legally usable for the time being. That said, the FCC has signaled it isn't done with this issue, so agents buying purchased data should treat scrubbing and consent documentation as standard practice, not optional overhead — the regulatory ground here can shift again with little warning.
