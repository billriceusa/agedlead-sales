The Link Budget

The newsletter already out-earns the website per unit of effort. It spends twenty of its twenty-one links sending readers home.

01The finding

Every issue is built from the same template. Here is where its links go.

Links in one issue 21 total  ·  2026-08-10 and 2026-08-17, identical
20 back to workagedleads.com 1 to agedleadstore.com — generic catalogue, 79% page depth

The one store link points at /all-lead-types/, the full catalogue, and sits at line 190 of a 241-line template — after six sections that all point back to our own site. Meanwhile lib/affiliate.ts maps 15 store buy pages by lead type. The newsletter uses none of them.

This is not a traffic problem. It is a placement problem, and it is fixable in one file.

02What one send is worth

The number that matters

One email produced 17 sessions. The whole website produces 5.1 per day. That single send was worth roughly 3.3 days of total site output — from one buried, generic link.

Email is already the highest-leverage channel we have. It is simply being run as a content newsletter rather than a revenue one.

Excluded on purpose

A fourth row, agedleadstore / email / registration_no_order, shows 130 sessions. That is Aged Lead Store mailing its own registrants — Troy's traffic, not a channel we operate. Counting it would overstate our contribution by about 30%.

03What has to be true to win

To be the #1 channel, the newsletter has to beat 154 sessions/month.

17 Sessions per send, today
36 Sessions per send needed
2.1× The required lift
188 Email per month, if hit

Weekly sends at today's yield give 74/month — already half the entire website's output. Getting to 36 per send puts the newsletter at 156/month; add the lifecycle drip's 32 and email reaches 188/month against the site's 154. Email becomes the #1 channel.

The 2.1× is not a guess — the site already proves it

The same audience, on our own domain, responds to banner placements far more than to inline text links:

Site placement Sessions (87d) Form
cta-banner 209 Standalone banner block
blog-post 88 Inline text links in body copy

Banners outperform inline links by 2.4× on-domain. The newsletter currently has zero banner placements above 79% depth. We are asking the email to do what the site already knows how to do.

04The CTA architecture

One file: lib/cron/newsletter-email.ts. Three placements instead of one.

Today

Header → Intro → Featured Article → Quick Tips → Industry Insight
  → This Week on the Blog → Flagship Playbook Strip → [STORE CTA] → Closing → Footer

Proposed

Header → Intro → [STORE CTA — above the fold] → Featured Article → Quick Tips
  → Industry Insight → This Week on the Blog → [VERTICAL SELF-SELECT STRIP]
  → [STORE CTA] → Closing → Footer
  1. Above the fold. A store CTA immediately after the intro. The reader who opens, skims, and leaves currently never sees a store link at all.
  2. Vertical self-select strip. Replace the generic catalogue link with four lead-type deep links built from storeCategoryPath(). The reader picks their own vertical. This displaces the Flagship Playbook Strip, which currently occupies prime real estate at 66% depth with three buttons pointing at our own site.
  3. Closing CTA. Keep the existing one, where it is.
Recommendation — don't segment the list

The audience is 2,584: Purchasers (~1,038), Inquiries (~1,267), newsletter-only (~226), store self-serve (~71). Genuinely different intents, and the instinct is to split the send.

Don't. The lifecycle drip already segments behaviourally; splitting the broadcast multiplies the approval burden on the one step that has already failed twice; and a self-select strip recovers most of the deep-link upside at a fraction of the operational cost. This keeps the standing "one unsegmented send" decision intact rather than reversing it. Revisit only after the CTA change has been measured.

05Three attribution defects

Two of these would have made the change look like it did nothing.

a. The success check reads the wrong row

The standing plan says to measure by comparing workagedleads / affiliate against the 5.6/day baseline. But newsletter clicks land under workagedleads / email. That check would show zero newsletter impact forever, no matter how well the newsletter performed.

b. utm_content carries the date, not the placement

Today it is utm_content=2026-08-10 — which tells you which issue, never which link. With three placements you could not tell which one earned the click. Fix: utm_content={date}-{placement}, giving 2026-08-10-hero, 2026-08-10-vertical-mortgage, 2026-08-10-footer. Keeps issue-level rollup, adds placement-level truth.

c. Resend exposes no engagement data through its API

The broadcast endpoint returns no delivered, opened, or clicked fields — open and click rates exist only in the Resend dashboard. GA4 store sessions are therefore the only programmatically verifiable measure of newsletter performance. Treat the GA4 row as the scoreboard and dashboard opens as context.

06The repeatable process

The design is sound. The execution has failed twice, both times at the same step.

  • 2026-08-10 — drafted 08-10, sent 08-12. Two days late.
  • 2026-08-17 — drafted 08-16, never sent, retired 08-27.

The Sunday cron drafts and previews correctly. The weak link is the manual send: no deadline, no reminder, no signal when an issue is sitting in limbo. An archived draft looks sendable forever — only the date ages.

  1. A killed flag with a refusal guard. Shipped this session. scripts/send-newsletter.ts now exits 1 on a retired issue, so no one can revive it by reaching for the newest archive file.
  2. An expiry line in the preview email. "Send by Wednesday or this issue retires." A draft with no deadline is a draft that rots.
  3. A newsletter:status check. Report any archive that is neither sent nor killed, wired into the existing health-check, so limbo is visible rather than silent.

07Decisions

Five calls. Recommendations attached to each.

MEMO 1

Three CTA placements, or keep one?

Three. This is the 2.1× lever — everything else in this document is downstream of it.

MEMO 2

Vertical self-select strip, or segmented sends?

Self-select. Keeps one send and preserves the standing decision.

MEMO 3

Which four verticals go in the strip?

Mortgage, final expense, auto, legal — but this should follow Troy's current inventory, not our guess. Worth one message to him.

MEMO 4

Displace the Flagship Playbook Strip?

Yes. It is prime real estate at 66% depth pointing at our own site.

MEMO 5

Hold the weekly cadence?

Weekly. The arithmetic in section 03 assumes it.

08The one input we don't have

Stated plainly, because it changes what "#1" means.

Ranking channels by revenue rather than sessions requires revenue per referred store session — conversion rate and commission. That number is not in GA4, not in the repo, and not in any system readable from here. Every figure in this document is denominated in sessions because that is the honest unit available.

Phase 0: get effective revenue per referred session from Troy. Until then, "the #1 affiliate revenue channel" is being measured by its best available proxy rather than by revenue itself.
