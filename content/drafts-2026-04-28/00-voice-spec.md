# Bill Rice voice spec — agedleadsales.com

Anchor every draft against this. Source: extracted from 5 published posts (mortgage-vs-trigger, refinance, broker scenario, aged-vs-real-time cost comparison, $2-vs-$50 economics).

## Stance & posture
- **Peer-to-peer with practitioners.** Reader is a working agent, broker, or producer — not a tourist. No 101 throat-clearing. Lead with the question they already have.
- **Receipts before opinion.** Numbers, then framing. Worked examples ("Agent A vs Agent B"), specific dollar amounts, real conversion math.
- **Calmly contrarian.** Calls out the industry's own narrative without being shrill. "Real-time lead vendors love to quote high conversion rates — and they're not lying, but they're not telling the whole story either."
- **Authority, lightly worn.** "25+ years," firsthand Quicken Loans references, "this has been tracked across thousands of campaigns." Never credentialing-spam.

## Opening pattern
The first 2-3 paragraphs frame the question the reader is already asking. Examples Bill uses:
- "Loan officers have two very different options for prospecting outside of realtor referrals…"
- "Every agent eventually asks the same question: 'Should I buy aged leads or real-time leads?'"
- "If you've decided to try aged leads, the next question is simple: where do you buy them?"

Avoid:
- "In this article…"
- "In today's competitive landscape…"
- "Are you struggling with…?"

## Cadence
- Short declarative sentences mixed with longer explanatory ones.
- 2-4 sentence paragraphs are the dominant unit.
- Em-dashes used freely for asides — Bill is comfortable with them — but never as filler.
- Sentence lengths vary: 8-15 words is the median, occasional 25-word sentences for nuance, occasional 4-word punches ("Read those numbers again.").

## Structural moves
- **Worked-example sections.** "Let me show you what this looks like with real numbers." Then concrete dollar math down to the decimal.
- **A vs B comparisons.** Agent A buys real-time, Agent B buys aged. Same budget, different outcome. This is the central rhetorical engine.
- **Section closers.** Each major section ends with a synthesis line or one-sentence punch — not a bulleted "key takeaway."
- **Practical takeaways near end.** A short list of action items the reader can run on Monday morning.
- **Soft CTA at the very end.** Affiliate or playbook link integrated as an offer to model the same economics, never "BUY NOW."

## Vocabulary fingerprint
Recurring phrases — use naturally, don't force:
- "Cost per closing" / "cost per acquisition" (CPA)
- "The math" — "the math is overwhelmingly in favor of…", "run the math"
- "Pipeline" (especially in mortgage context)
- "Cadence" (always for follow-up sequences, never "sequence" alone)
- "Speed-to-lead"
- "Competition-adjusted conversion rate"
- "Volume changes everything"
- "Aged" — never "old" (industry term respect)
- "Effective conversion rate" — distinct from quoted/marketing conversion rate
- "Sticker price" vs "real price"

Bill says "agents," "brokers," "loan officers," "producers" — never "users" or "customers" for the reader.

## Jargon handling
- Industry term gets a 5-10 word inline definition the first time it appears: "speed-to-lead — the idea that the first agent to call wins."
- After definition, just use the term. Don't re-define.
- Acronyms expanded once: "NCOA (National Change of Address)."

## Math conventions
- Concrete numbers, not ranges, in worked examples ("$500 budget. Lead cost: $35 each. Leads purchased: 14.").
- Use ranges when describing the market ("real-time leads run $20-75 depending on vertical").
- Show every step of multiplication. Reader should be able to plug their own numbers in.
- ROI expressed as percentage AND profit dollars — both, not one.

## Compliance framing
- Even-handed. Not alarmist. "Tightening regulatory environment" not "TCPA NIGHTMARE."
- Compliance is part of the operator's job, not a panic topic.
- TCPA references when relevant get a single sentence ("This is also why TCPA-compliant cadence matters — see [linked piece]"). Don't derail the article.
- The Henson Legal / Mode A-B-C framing belongs in compliance pieces specifically, not every article.

## Section format
- H2 = major topic shift. Around 6-10 H2s in a 2,500-word article.
- H3 = sub-points within an H2. Use sparingly.
- No "TL;DR" labels — Bill uses excerpt + KeyTakeawayBox component instead.
- No emoji. No emoji headers. No 🚀 or ✅ in any context.

## Affiliate / link conventions
- Internal links to existing pieces are dense and natural ("…see [aged refinance leads](/blog/...) for the full breakdown").
- AgedLeadStore.com gets a soft mention near the end, never in the lede, never repeated more than 2x in the article.
- Calculator embeds when they fit ("you can run your own numbers in the [ROI calculator](/calculators/roi)").
- Glossary tooltips fire automatically — don't force-link glossary terms.

## Article close
The closer is a "what this means for you" moment, then either:
1. A 5-7 line bulleted action list ("Start with $1,500 in aged leads, track cost per closing…"), OR
2. A paragraph that tees up a related decision ("If you want to model similar economics for your own business…") with the soft CTA inside.

Never end with "Hope this helps!" or "Let us know in the comments!" — there are no comments.

## Things Bill never does
- Never opens with a question ("Are you tired of…?").
- Never says "leverage" as a verb.
- Never uses "in conclusion" or "to wrap up."
- Never breaks the fourth wall ("As an SEO writer, I…").
- Never qualifies a number with "approximately around roughly" stack — picks one.
- Never uses "game-changer," "revolutionary," "secret weapon," "ninja," "rockstar."
- Never lists "Pros" / "Cons" boxes for tactical advice — only on review/comparison pieces.
- Never refuses to take a position. He has takes. He defends them with math.
