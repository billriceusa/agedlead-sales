# Article 3: How Much Do Life Insurance Leads Cost in 2026? Pricing by Product, Age, and Channel

## Target query
Primary: "how much do life insurance leads cost" (GSC-flagged)
Secondary: "life insurance leads cost", "life insurance leads pricing 2026", "buy life insurance leads", "term life leads cost", "final expense leads cost"

## Strategic role
Cost-intent piece, but more nuanced than auto because life insurance is really three sub-markets (term, final expense, IUL/HNW) with very different economics. The article needs to break out pricing by sub-vertical, then show the FYC-justified CPA for each.

## Hook / thesis
"Life insurance leads cost" is a misleading question. Term, final expense, and IUL leads are three different products with three different price ranges and three different commission structures. The right question is: what's the right CPA for the product I sell, and what tier of lead delivers it?

## Structure

### Opening (200 words)
The question "how much do life insurance leads cost" is the wrong question. There isn't one life-insurance-leads market. There are three: term life, final expense / burial, and IUL / high-net-worth. Pricing ranges from $0.62 to $80 per lead depending on which one you're buying. The relevant question — for any agent — is: what's the cost-per-acquisition that my product's commission structure justifies, and which tier of lead actually delivers it? This article walks the three sub-markets, the pricing in each, and the CPA math that decides where you should be buying.

### H2: Three sub-markets, three different price ranges
Brief framing of why life insurance lead pricing varies so much:
- Term: mid-market, low premiums, low FYC, high volume model
- Final expense: senior market, monthly premiums $30-80, FYC $500-1,500, telesales-heavy
- IUL: HNW, monthly premiums $300-1,000+, FYC $3,000-10,000+, consultative sales

Different consumers, different sales cycles, different CPA math. Pricing reflects that.

### H2: Term life leads — the volume play
- Pricing context
- Commission context: $40-150 monthly premium, $500-2,000 FYC depending on face amount
- CPA math: at $35/lead and 5% conversion = $700 CPA on real-time. At $8/aged lead and 1.5% conversion = $533 CPA. Aged wins, but margin is tight.
- Term life is the hardest of the three sub-markets to make work on real-time leads because the FYC ceiling caps what you can spend on acquisition.
- Aged term life works best as the entry product for a multi-product agency: term in, IUL or whole life conversion later.
- Link to existing aged life insurance scripts post.

### H2: Final expense leads — where direct mail still matters
- Pricing context. FE has the most lead-channel diversity: internet form, direct mail response, live transfer.
- Commission context: $30-80 monthly premiums, $500-1,500 FYC, often 110-120% target premium first year.
- The direct mail response sub-segment is unusual — it's expensive ($8-25 aged exclusive) but converts dramatically better because the consumer took an offline action.
- CPA math: $25 direct mail aged lead at 8% conversion = $313 CPA. $1.25 internet-form aged at 1% conversion = $125 CPA. Two different operational modes. Both can be profitable.
- The case for direct mail aged: lower call volume, higher per-conversation conversion, fewer DNC issues with senior demographic.
- Reference existing FE telesales post.

### H2: IUL leads — where the math gets interesting
- Pricing context. IUL is the highest-priced segment per lead but the highest commission per sale.
- Commission context: $3,000-10,000+ FYC. A single IUL closing can fund 6-12 months of lead spend.
- The aged IUL discount is enormous: $2-6 aged vs $40-75 real-time. CPA math favors aged dramatically because the commission ceiling is high enough to absorb either, and competition on aged IUL is essentially zero.
- IUL aged math: $5/lead × 1,000 leads = $5,000. 25% contact = 250 conversations. 1% conversion to closed IUL = 2.5 sales × $5,000 FYC = $12,500. Net: $7,500.
- Real-time IUL math: $50/lead × 100 leads = $5,000. Higher contact rate but speed-to-lead competition. 4% conversion = 4 sales × $5,000 = $20,000. Net: $15,000.
- Real-time wins on volume here, IF you have the speed-to-lead infrastructure. Most independent producers don't, which is why aged IUL is the smart play for solo.
- Reference existing IUL articles.

### H2: Why aged life insurance leads work even at older ages
Specific to life insurance: the underlying need (spouse, mortgage, kids, retirement income) doesn't expire on a 90-day timer. A 180-day-old final expense lead is still 70+ years old. A 6-month-old IUL lead is still building wealth.

That's structurally different from auto (where the policy renews every 6 months and the prospect may have already bought) or short-term mortgage purchase (where the home decision has resolved).

Implication: the price-to-conversion ratio holds at older ages in life insurance better than in any other vertical. 180-day-old leads at $1 each can be the most profitable single-lead-cost tier in the entire life market.

### H2: The compliance frame — different rules by sub-market
- Term and IUL: standard TCPA framework applies. Aged internet-form leads with original form-fill consent are fine.
- Final expense: senior demographic = higher TCPA risk profile. State mini-TCPAs (FL FTSA especially) impose tighter restrictions on calls to seniors.
- Cross-reference TCPA compliance article.

### H2: How to allocate your life insurance lead budget
For a multi-product life agency:
- 50% aged final expense (cheap, high volume, good for lead-bait conversion to other products)
- 25% aged IUL (highest CPA payoff, lowest competition)
- 15% aged term (gateway product, multi-product cross-sell potential)
- 10% real-time live transfer FE (specifically for telesales operations that can absorb the cost)

For a final-expense-only agency:
- 60% direct mail response aged (the highest-quality tier for true FE telesales)
- 30% aged internet-form (volume, lower CPA per close even at lower conversion rate)
- 10% live transfer real-time (premium close-day inventory)
