Loan officers who buy aged mortgage leads at $2–$8 per record already understand the economics. Life insurance agents are slower to make the same calculation — which is partly why the category is still priced at $0.62–$1.88 per aged lead when it probably should be three times that.

The hesitation is usually the same question: *do aged life insurance leads actually close?* They do. But the answer isn't uniform across product types. Term life, final expense, and IUL/whole life are three different businesses with three different CPA structures. Aged inventory works differently in each.

This article breaks out the math by sub-vertical, shows the 7-day cadence that produces those contact rates, and covers the one compliance variable that matters most coming out of 2025.

The aged price points look almost too cheap. That's intentional — aged inventory is priced for volume. The operating question is whether the lower conversion rate at that price still produces a lower cost-per-acquisition. The math usually says yes, decisively.

## The CPA math by sub-vertical

Run three scenarios on a $1,000 monthly lead budget, one per product type.

### Term life — the volume model

Agent A puts $1,000 into real-time shared term leads at $22 each. Forty-five leads. Contacted rate around 40%: 18 conversations. Close rate on contacted: 5%. Result: 0.9 closes. At a median term-life FYC of $800 on a $500K 20-year policy, that's $720 in revenue on $1,000 spent.

Agent B puts $1,000 into aged term leads at $1.25 each. Eight hundred leads. Contacted rate around 18%: 144 conversations. Close rate on contacted: 2%. Result: 2.9 closes. Same $800 FYC: $2,320 in revenue on $1,000 spent.

The conversion rate is one-third of real-time. The revenue is three times as high. Volume changes everything — and that's the insight most real-time buyers are still missing.

### Final expense — the cadence play

FE is different. The buyer demographic (55–75, often fixed income) rewards persistence and warmth more than speed. Real-time live transfers are standard in this vertical — and at $65 per transfer, the math can work at a 20% close rate. Three closes per $1,000 at $700 FYC = $2,100.

Aged FE leads at $1.25 each on a $1,000 budget buys 800 records. Contact rate for agents running a disciplined 5-touch phone + direct mail cadence: around 22%. Effective close rate on contacted: 2.5%. Result: 4.4 closes. At $700 FYC: $3,080.

The catch: you don't get that 22% contact rate from dialing alone. FE aged leads respond to direct mail as a primer. Budget $150–200 of your $1,000 for a postcard or yellow letter, send it on day five of the cadence, and your per-close economics still beat live transfers — but only with the multi-channel execution.

### IUL and whole life — the small-file, high-stakes model

IUL is where aged inventory's unit economics are most compelling, and where the quality of what you buy matters most.

IUL buyers were researching cash-value accumulation, tax-deferred growth, or retirement income strategies. An aged IUL lead at $3.50 represents someone who completed a high-intent form about a complex product. Real-time IUL leads run $40–$75 per shared record — which is why almost nobody runs real-time acquisition in this sub-vertical at volume.

A $1,000 budget at $3.50 per lead buys 285 records. Contact rate for a strong IUL operator: 20%. Close rate on contacted: 4%. Result: 2.3 closes. At a conservative $3,500 FYC: $8,050 in revenue on $1,000 spent. Run your own numbers in the [ROI calculator](/calculators/roi-calculator).

That math is why agents who figure out IUL on aged inventory tend to stop buying any other inventory.

## What's actually in an aged life insurance lead

The fields included vary by provider, but a quality aged life insurance record should carry: full name, phone (validated), email, address, product type requested (term, whole, FE, or universal), and optionally coverage amount, current policy status, and date of birth.

Four fields predict conversion probability more than the others:

- **Coverage amount entered.** A consumer who typed "500,000" was running a specific calculation. One who left it blank or selected a default was window-shopping.
- **Product specificity.** "20-year term" beats "life insurance" in close rate. Specific intent = real decision in progress.
- **Phone validation.** Disconnected number = zero conversion. If your provider doesn't validate at delivery, use a phone validation service before import. Don't dial a file with unvalidated numbers.
- **Source tier.** Aggregator-sourced leads (PolicyGenius, SelectQuote, EverQuote) convert 1.5–2x better than affiliate co-registration forms. Worth paying 15–20% more per record to filter for this.

## Score the file before you dial

Sorting a 500-record aged file in arrival order is the slowest possible path to revenue. The high-probability records — specific coverage amount, validated mobile, aggregator source, 30–45 days old — are scattered randomly through the file. Call them first.

A workable scoring model: weight coverage specificity (1–5 points), phone validation (0 or 5), lead age bracket (31–45 days scores 5, 46–85 scores 3, 86–180 scores 1), and source tier (1–5). Sort descending, dial in score order. If you're running 2,000+ records per month, the [AI scoring methodology](/blog/ai-lead-scoring-aged-leads) is worth building out properly.

## A 7-day cadence for life insurance aged leads

Contact rates for aged life insurance leads peak in the first seven days. The conversion curve flattens hard after that. Run this cadence — don't ad-lib it.

**Day 1, morning:** Call. Leave a 20-second voicemail if no answer: *"Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Agency] — you'd looked into [term/life] coverage a while back. I had a quick question about what you found. Take 60 seconds when you get this."* No product pitch in the voicemail. Ask a question.

**Day 1, two hours later:** Email. One paragraph. Mirrors the voicemail tone exactly. Subject line: *"Following up on your life insurance question."*

**Day 2, afternoon:** Call again. Different time window from day one. No voicemail on the second attempt — it creates pressure. If they answer, you've been trying to reach them for two days without being pushy about it.

**Day 4, morning:** Call + text (mobile validated leads only). The text: *"Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] re: your life insurance inquiry. Let me know if you have a few minutes this week."* Texts have a 3× open rate versus email. Use them.

**Day 5:** For FE and IUL leads, drop a mail piece — letter or postcard. It arrives around days 8–10 and serves as a touchpoint after your first calling pass completes. A $0.60 postcard on a lead that cost $1.25 is still a $1.85 total acquisition cost.

**Day 7:** Final call + email. Subject line: *"Last check-in on your coverage question."* Give them an explicit out: *"If this isn't a priority right now, no worries — just let me know and I'll stop reaching out."* Agents who give people a clean exit close better than agents who don't. The buyer relaxes and responds.

After day 7, move to a 30-day resurrection list. Call monthly. Life insurance needs don't disappear — new baby, new home, employer plan change, health scare — they pause. A meaningful percentage of FE and IUL closes come from the 60–90 day resurrection calls on leads that went cold in the first pass.

## One compliance checkpoint

The FCC 1:1 consent rule — which would have required separate written consent naming each specific vendor at the time of original opt-in — was vacated in January 2025 and fully repealed in August 2025. For most aged internet-form inventory, this means the prior-consent framework applies: written consent obtained when the consumer filled out the original form covers calls from licensed agents purchasing that data.

What's still active and expanding: state-level mini-TCPAs. Florida (FTSA), Oklahoma (OTSA), Washington (HB 1497), Maryland, and Texas have enacted laws that go materially further than the federal baseline on call frequency, time windows, and opt-out handling. If you're dialing in any of these states, your cadence needs to reflect that.

## What to filter for when you buy

Not all aged life insurance inventory is created equal. The filters that matter:

- **Age bracket.** 31–85 days is the sweet spot. The consumer has cycled out of the "five agents called me this week" window, but the data is fresh enough to work. Past 180 days, contact rates drop another 30%.
- **Product type.** Buy term, FE, and IUL separately. They need different scripts, different dialing approaches, different close timelines. Mixing them in one import creates operational confusion.
- **Geography.** Buy in states where you're licensed. New operators sometimes buy national lists and limit themselves to a fraction of the inventory they paid for.
- **Volume.** Start with 200–300 records before scaling. A $300 test tells you more about your scripts and CRM setup than anything else in this business.
