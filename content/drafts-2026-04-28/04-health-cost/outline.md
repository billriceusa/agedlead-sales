### H2: The CMS TPMO consent rule — why aged Medicare Advantage isn't what it used to be
This is the section that earns the article.

Background:
- CMS final rule CMS-4205-F (CY 2025), effective October 1, 2024
- Applies to all TPMOs (third-party marketing organizations) — including independent agents and brokers as well as lead generators
- Requires "prior express written consent" before a TPMO shares a beneficiary's personal data with another TPMO for MA/PDP marketing or enrollment
- Consent must be obtained SEPARATELY for each downstream TPMO that receives the data
- Blanket "I agree to be contacted by partners" consent is no longer sufficient
- Carried forward into CY 2026 rule (CMS-4208-F)

Operational implication:
- Many "aged Medicare Advantage leads" sold today were originated under the old broad-consent regime
- If you're a TPMO contacting a Medicare beneficiary for MA/PDP enrollment, the original blanket consent may not cover your contact under CMS rules
- You can still call the lead under TCPA (the original form-fill consent travels with the record), but the CMS overlay specifically restricts WHAT you can do during the call without separate per-TPMO consent

The clean paths in 2026:
- Aged Medicare leads originated post-October 2024 with TPMO-specific consent on the original form: cleanest
- Aged Medicare Supplement leads: not regulated by CMS the same way (MedSupp falls outside the MA/PDP TPMO framework) — substantially more flexible
- Final Expense aged leads to Medicare-eligible seniors: clean, but you're selling FE not MA
- ACA aged leads: outside the CMS framework entirely

### H2: ACA / Marketplace leads — the cleanest aged inventory
- Pricing context: $0.50-$0.94 aged shared, vs $20-$40 real-time shared
- OEP cycle: Nov 1 - Jan 15 each year. Q4 demand spike → real-time prices climb 20-40%
- SEP year-round: birth, marriage, divorce, job loss, geographic move, etc. Aged ACA leads are valuable year-round because SEP demand is constant.
- Operational play: buy aged ACA leads from Q1-Q2 to call during Q3-Q4. Demand ramps as OEP approaches; aged inventory is cheapest right after the prior OEP closes (March-April).
- CPA math worked example.

### H2: Medicare Supplement leads — the underrated aged category
- Pricing context: $1-$4 aged shared, $25-$50 real-time shared
- Year-round market with key timing: Initial Enrollment Period (turning 65), Birthday Rule states (CA, OR, WA, ID, KY, LA, MD, ME, MO, NV, OK), Guaranteed Issue periods
- NOT regulated by the CMS TPMO marketing rule (MedSupp is governed by state insurance commissioners and standardized federal Plan letters, but the TPMO marketing rule specifically applies to MA/PDP)
- This makes aged MedSupp the most operationally clean Medicare-segment aged category in 2026
- CPA math: at $3/lead and 3% conversion = $100 CPA, against typical $400-700 first-year MedSupp commission
- Strategic play: lead with MedSupp, cross-sell or upgrade to MA when appropriate timing/eligibility

### H2: Medicare Advantage leads — the regulated path
- Pricing context: $1.50-$8 aged shared, $30-$150 real-time
- CMS TPMO consent rule means you have to verify the original form consent language carefully
- Reputable aged providers should now be tagging Medicare leads with original consent regime (pre-Oct 2024 vs post-Oct 2024) and the specific TPMO consent language on the form
- If your provider can't or won't give you that data, you're taking compliance risk you may not understand
- Year-round leads OK for marketing communications and rate-shop conversations, but enrollment-related contact requires TPMO-specific consent
- Practical workflow: many MA agents now use aged MA leads for educational outreach and informational calls, with formal enrollment moving to a separate consent-capture step
- CPA math factors in the longer compliance-aware sales cycle.

### H2: The math — three Medicare agents, same $1,000 budget
**Agent A — buys real-time MA leads at $50/lead**
- 20 leads, speed-to-lead pressure, effective conversion 8% = 1.6 enrollments. At $300 first-year commission per enrollment + $300 renewal: gross $480 + $480 = $960 total. Net of $1,000: -$40. Loses on year one, breakeven year two.

**Agent B — buys aged MA leads at $4/lead, post-Oct 2024 with TPMO consent**
- 250 leads, 25% contact = 62 conversations. Compliance-aware conversion 3% = 1.9 enrollments. Net: $920 year one revenue.

**Agent C — buys aged MedSupp leads at $3/lead**
- 333 leads, 30% contact = 100 conversations. Compliance is simpler, conversion 4% = 4 sales. At $500 FYC: $2,000 gross. Net: $1,000.

Not unique to MedSupp — but the cleaner regulatory environment and longer commission tail (avg renewal stickiness on MedSupp is high) makes the math notably better than MA for solo agents in 2026.

### H2: Open enrollment timing — when to buy and when to call
- ACA OEP: Nov 1 - Jan 15. Buy aged April-July, call September-January.
- Medicare AEP: Oct 15 - Dec 7. Buy aged Feb-April, call August-December.
- Medicare MA-OEP: Jan 1 - Mar 31 (existing MA enrollees can switch). Buy aged Sep-Nov, call Jan-Mar.
- Medicare Supplement: year-round, birthday-rule states matter for timing
- ACA SEP: triggered by life events year-round

The aged-leads timing arbitrage in health insurance is one of the strongest in any vertical. Prices drop dramatically right after each enrollment period closes; demand rises right before each one opens. Buying in the trough and calling in the peak is a real strategic edge.

### H2: How to vet a health insurance lead provider in 2026
Specific to this vertical:
- For Medicare Advantage: ask for original-form consent language and the date the form was filled (pre or post Oct 2024 CMS rule)
- For all health: TCPA documentation including original disclosure URL
- Bad-data rate: should be under 15% on legitimate aged inventory
- Return policy: 20% standard
- Filtering by carrier (some MA buyers want to avoid the major carriers' captive consumers)
- State-specific filtering for ACA (state-based exchanges differ from federal exchange)
