## Structure

### Opening (200 words)
Frame the sticker-shock dynamic. An agent comparing real-time vendor sites sees $30-75 per lead and assumes that's the market. Most agents stop there. The actual market — including aged, exclusive, shared, live transfer — spans $0.25 to $100. Cost per acquisition (CPA), not cost per lead (CPL), is what determines whether a $35 lead or a $0.40 lead is the better buy. The article walks the full pricing table and runs the CPA math for both.

### H2: The 2026 auto insurance lead pricing table
Full table, all tiers, current site benchmarks + cross-references:
- Shared real-time internet form: $20-45 (site), $10-30 (broader market)
- Exclusive real-time: $50-100 (site), $25-100 (market)
- Live transfer: $25-75
- Aged 31-85 days shared: $0.25-0.50
- Aged 86-180 days shared: $0.25-0.50 (price floor — minimal additional discount past 90 days because supply is large)

Brief commentary on each tier — what you're buying, what the contact and conversion rates look like, which vendor types dominate that tier.

### H2: What drives the price spread
Five factors explain why the same data point sells for $0.40 or $40:
- **Exclusivity**: shared (3-8 buyers) vs exclusive (1 buyer)
- **Age**: minutes vs days vs months
- **Source quality**: aggregator (LendingTree, EverQuote) vs niche site vs affiliate networks
- **Filtering**: ZIP, state, age band, prior coverage, SR-22, accident history
- **Channel format**: internet form vs live transfer vs direct mail response vs Facebook lead gen

Each factor compounds. A 90-day-old shared internet-form lead with no filters from a small niche site costs $0.25. A real-time exclusive live transfer with prior-coverage filtering and a verified phone number costs $75. Same consumer demographic. Different product engineering.

### H2: Why cost per lead is the wrong metric
Cost per acquisition is the only number that matters. CPL × leads-needed-to-close = CPA.
For shared real-time: high CPL × low effective conversion (5-buyer competition compresses real conversion) = $400-700 CPA.
For aged: low CPL × moderate-low conversion × higher volume = $80-200 CPA.
Worked example with auto-specific numbers.

### H2: The math — two auto agents, same $1,000 budget
**Agent A — buys shared real-time at $30/lead**
- 33 leads, 80% answer rate within 5 min = 26 contacts
- Sold to 5 agents, effective conversion on shared real-time auto = ~6% of contacts (industry average accounts for the multi-quote behavior)
- Closings: 26 × 0.06 = 1.6 sales
- At $400 first-year commission (typical 6-month auto policy + LTV expectation): gross $640. Net of $1,000 lead spend: -$360. Loses money.

**Agent B — buys aged at $0.40/lead**
- 2,500 leads, 25% cumulative contact rate over 14-day cadence = 625 contacts
- Conversion rate on contacts (no real-time competition, longer pre-call prep time): 2.5%
- Closings: 625 × 0.025 = 15.6 sales
- At $400 FYC: gross $6,240. Net: $5,240. ROI: 524%.

Same product. Same effort. Different math. The only reason Agent A's model survives at all is renewal commissions and cross-sell — both of which Agent B captures more of because she has 10x the policies.

### H2: When real-time auto leads do make sense
Be fair. Real-time isn't worthless. It's worthless for solo agents working shared inventory at scale. It works in three specific cases:
- **Niche underwriting**: SR-22, non-standard, high-risk where the auction is thinner
- **High-LTV captive carrier writing**: when you're writing 4-line bundled policies and your retention math justifies a $30 acquisition cost
- **Speed-to-lead competitive advantage**: auto-dialer + dedicated inbound team beating the rest of the auction

Most independent P&C agents fail to meet any of these criteria. The math in the worked example above is closer to their reality.

### H2: How telematics and the modern auto market changed lead economics
Carrier-direct telematics programs (Progressive Snapshot, State Farm Drive Safe & Save, Allstate Drivewise) have cherry-picked the best auto risks out of the open market. What's left in the third-party lead pool is increasingly skewed toward:
- Non-standard / high-risk drivers
- Drivers with recent accidents or violations
- SR-22 filings
- Drivers shopping because they got non-renewed
- Younger / first-time buyers without telematics history

That's not bad news. Those are exactly the segments where independent agents (especially non-standard specialists) win. But it means the "average auto lead" the agent buys today is qualitatively different from the average auto lead five years ago. Pricing your conversions against modern data is more important than ever.

### H2: Aged auto lead pricing by age cohort
Brief breakdown of why pricing flattens past 90 days:
- 8-30 days: $1-3 (some retain real-time freshness premium)
- 31-85 days: $0.25-0.50
- 86-180 days: $0.25-0.50
- 181-365 days: $0.20-0.40

Past 90 days, the pricing floor is set by aged-marketplace bulk economics, not by perceived freshness value. There's almost no difference in price between a 100-day-old lead and a 250-day-old lead. There IS a difference in cumulative conversion, but most agents over-estimate how much.

### H2: Cross-sell math — why the aged auto strategy compounds
Auto is the gateway product. Most P&C agents who close an auto policy can roll it into multi-line bundles (home, renters, umbrella, life). Aged auto is the cheapest top-of-funnel for that funnel. Run the LTV math against an aged auto lead at $0.40 instead of a real-time at $30:
- Agent A: 1.6 auto sales/month × 30% multi-line bundle rate × $200 incremental commission = $96 in cross-sell
- Agent B: 15.6 auto sales/month × 30% × $200 = $936 in cross-sell

That's the underrated argument for aged auto. Reference [Auto Insurance Aged Leads: The Cross-Sell Strategy That Doubles Revenue](/blog/auto-insurance-aged-leads-cross-sell-strategy-double-revenue).

### H2: How to read provider auto pricing — what to look for
Before buying, get answers to:
- What's the source of the original form fill? (Aggregator, niche site, social, mobile app?)
- What's the bad-data rate? (Disconnected, deceased, wrong number, duplicate.)
- What's the return policy? (Up to 20% credit is industry standard for legitimate providers.)
- Can you filter by SR-22, prior coverage, ZIP, vehicle count, prior carrier?
- Is the lead exclusive at the price quoted, or shared by default?
- What TCPA documentation can they produce on request?

A provider that won't give you all of these answers is a provider you don't buy from.

### H2: Practical buying playbook
- Start with $500 in aged 31-85 day shared, filtered to state and high-risk ZIPs
- Build 14-day cadence, 6 touches
- Track cost per contact, cost per quoted, cost per bound, and cost per multi-line
- Run a 60-day review against the worked-example numbers above
- Scale or refilter based on actual conversion data, not gut feel
