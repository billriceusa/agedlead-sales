## Hook / thesis
The trigger-lead ban that took effect March 5, 2026 didn't just kill one acquisition channel — it eliminated the largest non-referral, non-internet-form mortgage lead source in the industry. That makes aged internet-form mortgage leads the dominant low-cost prospecting channel for loan officers in 2026, and changes the math for every broker and LO who has been buying real-time leads.

### H2: What aged mortgage leads actually are
- 100-word definition
- 30-365 day-old internet-form submissions (Bankrate, LendingTree, Zillow upstream, then resold)
- Same consumer data as real-time, just delivered after the original auction window
- Distinction: aged INTERNET-FORM leads (legal, opt-in, internet-form CDN-tracked) vs aged trigger leads (now banned vs the ban is on third-party trigger sales)
- Distinction: purchase intent vs refinance intent — both age, refis age better

### H2: The 2026 reality — why aged mortgage leads matter more than they did 12 months ago
- Trigger ban: paragraph on Homebuyers Privacy Protection Act, S.1467/H.R.2808, effective March 5, 2026
- What loan officers lost: the $0.15-0.50 daily credit-pull cold list
- What displaced trigger buyers will do: enter the real-time auction → upward price pressure on $30-100 internet-form leads
- Aged internet-form leads are the only channel where supply hasn't shrunk and prices haven't moved up
- Soft caveat: trigger leads are not entirely gone — current servicer/banking relationships still allowed; this is about cold third-party purchase

### H2: Purchase leads vs refinance leads — the aging curve is different
- Purchase aged leads = decaying intent (buyer found a home or stopped looking) — 60-90 day half-life
- Refinance aged leads = persistent intent — every rate move is fresh hook
- Why refi aged leads are the most undervalued: link to existing post
- Specific guidance: in 2026, 60-80% of an aged batch should be refi-skewed if your pricing engine supports both

### H2: The math — two loan officers, same $1,500 budget
- Worked example A vs B, in Bill's voice
- LO A: real-time at $50/lead = 30 leads, 40% contact rate (sold to 5 LOs, effective contact 20%), 4% close rate on contacts = ~0.24 closings → 0 closings most months, 1 closing some months
- LO B: aged 31-85 day at $4/lead = 375 leads, 25% cumulative contact over 14-day cadence = 94 conversations, 2.5% close rate on contacts = 2.3 closings
- Same budget. Same product. Different math.
- Cost per closing comparison: $6,250 (best case for LO A) vs $652 (LO B)
- At $3,500 average commission per closed loan: LO A = $0-3,500 revenue against $1,500 spend; LO B = $8,000+ against $1,500 spend.

### H2: Where aged mortgage leads come from
- Origination flow: consumer fills internet form → quote engine sells real-time to 3-5 buyers → unsold/un-worked tail goes to aged inventory after 7-14 days
- Why the lead is still valuable at day 60-90: original buyers gave up after 1-2 calls. Most haven't bought. Most still have the same problem.
- Two-tier market structure: aggregators (LendingTree, Bankrate) → wholesale to aged marketplaces (AgedLeadStore, etc.) → direct LO/broker buyers

### H2: The compliance frame — TCPA, the trigger ban, and aged leads
- Trigger ban does NOT touch aged internet-form leads. Different statute, different consent basis.
- Aged internet-form leads carry the original consumer's TCPA consent at the time of form fill. Verify the original consent language in your provider's TCPA documentation.
- FCC 1:1 rule vacated/repealed (Aug 2025), but that's about consent specificity for new lead forms — doesn't retroactively affect aged inventory.
- Revocation rule (10 business days, "any reasonable means") fully applies — your CRM has to honor STOPs and DNC adds in real time.
- Reference TCPA compliance article for the full framework. Brief, not derailing.

### H2: Buying your first batch — practical playbook
- Pick the age tier that matches your budget and conversion expectation
- Filter recommendations: state, ZIP, mortgage purpose (purchase vs refi), property type if your provider offers it
- Volume math: at $4/lead and a $1,500 budget you're buying ~375 records — enough sample for the law of averages
- Cadence: 14-day touch sequence (link to outreach cadence calculator + scripts post)
- Tools: CRM with bulk import, dialer with local presence, opt-out tracking
- First-month KPIs: 25%+ cumulative contact rate, 1-3% close on contacts, < $750 cost per closing

### H2: Common mistakes that kill aged mortgage lead campaigns
- Buying once, calling once. (Aged leads compound over 90 days.)
- Mixing purchase and refi in the same script.
- Not segmenting by age. (30-day leads get the first dial pass, not the 180-day stuff.)
- Ignoring rate-driven re-engagement triggers on refi.
- Treating aged like real-time and demanding sub-5-minute speed.
- Not tracking cost per closing — only tracking cost per contact.

### H2: How aged mortgage leads fit into your 2026 acquisition stack
- 50% referrals (always)
- 30% aged internet-form (the new core)
- 15% real-time (selective — best when targeted by specific scenario, e.g., you have a niche product real-time leads convert well into)
- 5% offline (direct mail, networking)
- Frame: stack diversification protects against single-channel rate sensitivity
