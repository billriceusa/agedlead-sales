## Sub-vertical playbooks

### Auto insurance

**Prospect intent:** lower premium, new vehicle, policy-renewal shopping.

**The 3-month-later pitch:** most people who shopped auto 3+ months ago didn't actually switch. They got a quote, felt it was marginal, and stayed put. Meanwhile their current carrier probably raised rates at renewal. The conversation opener: "What's your current premium doing since you last shopped?"

**Sub-segments:**
- Multi-vehicle households (easier cross-sell, higher ARPC)
- Renters + auto (bundle potential)
- Young drivers (20–30), often rate-sensitive, high churn

### Home insurance

**Prospect intent:** rate shopping, post-purchase (first-time owners), mortgage-lender-required quotes.

**The 3-month-later pitch:** home rates have been moving sharply in many states (FL, CA, TX) due to catastrophe re-rating. Prospects who shopped 3–6 months ago may have just gotten a non-renewal notice from their current carrier. Open with: "Have you seen what home rates did in your area this year?"

**Sub-segments:**
- Post-closing new buyers (often bundle-ready)
- Non-renewal recipients — high urgency, motivated
- HOA-mandated quote shoppers

### Term life insurance

**Prospect intent:** new baby, mortgage protection, spousal request, estate planning.

**The 3-month-later pitch:** term life needs rarely evaporate. If a prospect looked and didn't close, it's usually because they got confused by medical underwriting or didn't want to deal with a medical exam. New no-medical products (instant approval up to certain face amounts) have changed the conversation. Lead with that.

**Sub-segments:**
- Young parents (highest need, often lowest price sensitivity when framed correctly)
- Mortgage-holders without policies
- Seniors shopping simplified issue

### Final expense

**Prospect intent:** burial cost coverage, small whole-life policy, often senior-driven.

**The 3-month-later pitch:** the senior market for final expense is hot. Prospects who shopped 3–6 months ago are often still unprotected. They may be healthier, same health, or declined — all three are workable.

**Important:** final expense requires an empathetic, unhurried script. Never high-pressure. The demographic is older, often grief-touched, and remembers who treated them with dignity. Cadence rule: 2-week minimum between nurture touches on final expense. Weekly touches feel like harassment to this demographic.

### Medicare

**Prospect intent:** Medicare Advantage, Medigap, Part D, dual-eligible.

**The AEP cycle:** aged Medicare leads are highly cyclical. Your highest-value window is Oct 15–Dec 7 (Annual Enrollment Period). Prospects who shopped in Apr–Aug are often the best AEP candidates — they were curious but not ready to switch outside of AEP.

**Sub-segments:**
- New-to-Medicare (age 64.5–65) — longest-tail nurture, convert over months
- Current MAPD plan holders approaching AEP
- Dual-eligible (Medicare + Medicaid) — special enrollment periods, higher ARPC

**CMS compliance note:** Medicare marketing has the strictest compliance in the industry. Scope-of-appointment forms, recorded consent for telephone enrollment, specific disclaimers in all materials. This overlay is a starting point — invest in a Medicare-specific compliance review before outreach.

### Health (ACA / under-65)

**Prospect intent:** ACA plan shopping, supplemental, small business.

**The OEP cycle:** similar to Medicare, health has cyclical windows. Nov 1–Jan 15 is peak. Off-cycle leads often qualify for special enrollment periods (SEP) — life events that trigger re-shopping.

## Insurance-specific scripts

### Script IA1 — First-touch call (auto)

> "Hey [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Company]. A couple months back you shopped auto insurance quotes — just checking in to see if you ended up switching, or if your premium's still going up with your current carrier."

The "still going up" framing is loaded — it presumes their current carrier has raised rates, which is true ~70% of the time over a 3-month window in the current market.

### Script IA2 — First-touch call (home, non-renewal hook)

> "Hey [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Company]. You shopped home insurance back in [month] — wanted to check in. Did you end up switching, or are you still with your current carrier? I'm asking because a lot of folks in [state] have been getting non-renewal notices lately, and I wanted to make sure you're covered either way."

### Script IA3 — First-touch call (final expense, senior)

> "Hey [Name], this is [Your Name]. Back in [month] you asked for info on final expense coverage — I'm just checking in to see if you ever got that handled, or if it's still open. No pressure at all — just closing the loop."

Notice the tone shift. Softer, more deferential, no assumptive language. Finals prospects will hang up on the auto script and open up on this one.

### Script IA4 — First-touch call (Medicare, pre-AEP)

> "Hey [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Company]. You looked at Medicare plans back in [month] — with AEP coming up [in X weeks / starting Oct 15], I wanted to reach out and see if you're happy with your current plan or if you'd want me to walk you through your options for this year's enrollment."

**Scope of appointment.** For Medicare specifically, you cannot begin a plan-specific conversation on a cold call without a documented Scope of Appointment (SOA) — typically a signed or recorded consent that the prospect wants to discuss Medicare plans. Build this into your workflow.

### Script IA5 — First-touch voicemail (generic insurance)

> "Hey [Name] — [Your Name] with [Company]. Calling about the [auto/home/life/Medicare] insurance question you had back in [month]. Wanted to see if you got it squared away or if you still have options open. Give me a ring at [number] or I'll try you in a couple days."

### Insurance objection library

**"I already switched."** → "Nice — glad you got it sorted. Out of curiosity, how long ago and with who?" Nurture question, not a sales question. You're mapping their renewal cycle for a future touch.

**"I'm happy with my current coverage."** → "That's great. Can I ask — when's your next renewal? If I could save you 15% at that point would it be worth a 5-min chat then?" Set the future touch. Put it in the calendar.

**"My current company has been fine."** → "Totally fair. Quick question — have they raised rates in the last year? A lot of folks don't realize how much their premium moved because it just comes out automatically." Often the prospect will realize they haven't checked.

**"Send me a quote in writing."** → "Happy to — need 3 pieces of info to make it accurate: [appropriate fields]. Can I grab those real quick so the quote you get is actually useful?" If they refuse the info, they're fishing; move to lower-intensity nurture.

## Insurance SMS scripts

### Script IS1 — Auto, value-first

> "Hey [Name] — [Your Name] with [Company]. Auto rates moved a lot this year. On the profile from your quote back in [month], folks like you are saving $[range]/mo. Want me to double-check your exact numbers? Reply STOP to opt out."

### Script IS2 — Home, non-renewal hook

> "Hey [Name] — [Your Name] with [Company]. In [state] a lot of folks have been getting non-renewal notices. Just wanted to make sure you're still covered since you shopped home back in [month]. 2-min call to confirm? Reply STOP to opt out."

### Script IS3 — Medicare, AEP warm-up

> "Hey [Name] — AEP (Medicare annual enrollment) starts [date]. Wanted to get on your calendar for a quick plan review since you looked at options back in [month]. When works? Reply STOP to opt out."

### Script IS4 — Final expense (soft)

> "Hey [Name] — [Your Name] with [Company]. Just checking in on the [final expense / life] question you had a while back. Whenever you want to pick it up, let me know. Reply STOP to opt out."

Final expense SMS should always be softer, shorter, and lower-frequency. 1 SMS per week maximum on this demographic.

**Subject:** Two things to check on your home insurance

>

>
> 1. What does your renewal look like this year vs last? If it's up more than 10%, you have options.
> 2. Are you still covered for the correct replacement value? Materials costs have moved a lot.
>
> Want me to pull quotes on your address? I can have something to you in 30 minutes.
>
> — Bill

> If it's still something you want to look at, I can walk you through what most folks your age end up with, and it's typically a much quicker process than people expect.

## Compliance notes specific to insurance

### Medicare — the strict one

Medicare marketing is the most tightly regulated sub-vertical in insurance. CMS requires:

- **Scope of Appointment (SOA)** — documented consent to discuss plan specifics, signed or recorded 48 hours in advance (exceptions apply for inbound calls and walk-ins).
- **Licensed producer language** — specific disclaimers on all marketing materials.
- **No cold calls for Medicare Advantage or PDP plans** — unless the prospect explicitly requested contact. Aged Medicare leads need to trace back to an explicit request-for-contact.
- **Recorded telephonic enrollment** — full recording required for any Medicare Advantage or PDP enrollment completed over the phone.

**Practical implication:** treat Medicare leads as a separate channel with a separate compliance workflow. Do not run Medicare outreach off your general cadence. CMS audit risk is real and fines are real.

### Final expense — targeting and SSI

Final expense marketing to seniors is held to UDAP-like standards, plus whatever your state insurance department layers on top. Specific risks:

- No implied urgency. "Rates are about to go up" on an evergreen product is a trap.
- No implying government endorsement. No language that suggests the policy is a government program.
- Respect state-specific senior-marketing rules (several states have enhanced protections for senior insurance marketing).

### State DOI licensing

Every state where your prospects sit requires you to be licensed in that state for the line of business you're pitching. Your aged lead file will often include multi-state prospects. Before you call into a state, confirm you're licensed there. Outreach into a state you're not licensed in is both a marketing risk and a licensing violation.

### TCPA as always

Same TCPA rules as everything else — DNC scrub, PEWC for autodialer/SMS to cell phones, time windows in the prospect's local time zone (8 PM end in FL/OK/WA/MD/AL/CT/LA/MA/MS/WY). Final expense prospects in particular often file TCPA complaints — assume every number will be scrub-enforced and that you may need to produce consent documentation on demand.

**On the vacated 1:1 consent rule:** as of 2026, the 11th Circuit vacated (Jan 2025) and the FCC repealed (Aug 2025) the proposed 1-to-1 consent rule. PEWC is the standard in force. Inherited consent still works for manual dial + plain-text email; it is weaker for automated SMS, so the operator's move is Fresh-Consent Ladder (master playbook, Part IV).

### Insurance-specific fresh-consent plays

- **Rate-alert opt-in** (auto/home) — "Text SAVE to [number] and I'll let you know when I see a better rate for your profile." Works because most auto/home shoppers are passively rate-sensitive.
- **Renewal-reminder opt-in** — "When's your next renewal? Text me the month and I'll send a quote 30 days out." High intent, clean consent.
- **Final expense info packet** — gated behind a PEWC form, mailed physically. Seniors prefer physical mail and the form capture is pristine.
- **Pre-AEP Medicare review booking** — booked through a scheduling link with explicit SOA language built into the booking confirmation. Meets CMS Scope-of-Appointment rules AND creates fresh PEWC.
- **SEP event alerts** (ACA) — "Had a life event? Text CHANGE to [number] — I'll walk you through whether it opens a Special Enrollment Period." High-intent opt-in.

### Health (ACA) — CMS navigator rules

If you're working on-exchange ACA plans, CMS rules around navigator/agent/broker marketing apply. Disclosures required. Off-exchange short-term medical has a separate compliance regime — know which one you're in.

> **When in doubt:** [Henson Legal](https://www.henson-legal.com/). For Medicare questions specifically, confirm your attorney has current CMS marketing guidance experience — rules are refreshed annually with the CMS Medicare Marketing Guidelines.

## Insurance-specific nurture content

Build your nurture library by sub-vertical:

**Auto:**
- Quarterly rate-trend update
- Bundling savings calculator
- Seasonal (winter driving, summer road trips)
- Telematics / safe-driving program explainers

**Home:**
- Annual replacement-cost calculator
- Non-renewal / hard-market update
- Seasonal maintenance checklists (winterization, hurricane prep)
- HOA / mortgage-required coverage updates

**Life / final expense:**
- Simplified vs. traditional underwriting explainer
- Beneficiary designation refresh prompt
- Tax considerations (state-specific)
- "What recently widowed people wish they had done" content

**Medicare:**
- Pre-AEP calendar (July–Sept)
- AEP window reminders (Oct 15, Dec 7)
- SEP triggers explainer (moves, income changes, employer coverage loss)
- Low-income subsidy (LIS/Extra Help) screening
