## Email 3 — Day 4 (scripts + cadence)

**Subject:** The 14-day cadence — tuned for your line

**Preheader:** Auto vs home vs final expense — different scripts, same structure.

---

{{firstName}},

The 14-day opener cadence from the playbook is the same across insurance sub-verticals. What changes is the **script** and the **tone**.

**Cadence spine:**

- Day 1: Call (AM) + voicemail + SMS
- Day 2: Email
- Day 3: Call (PM) + SMS
- Day 5: Call (evening) + Email
- Day 7: Call + break-up SMS
- Day 10: Break-up email
- Day 14: Final call

**Script variations by line:**

**Auto — the "still going up" opener:**
> "Hey [Name], Bill with [Company]. A couple months back you shopped auto quotes — just checking in. Did you end up switching, or is your premium still going up with your current carrier?"

Works because 70%+ of drivers have seen a rate increase over a 3-month window. The opener presumes their pain.

**Home — the non-renewal hook:**
> "Hey [Name], Bill with [Company]. You shopped home insurance back in [month]. With what's been going on with rates in [state], I wanted to check in — are you still with your current carrier, or did you get non-renewal'd?"

Works because FL, CA, TX, and a dozen other states are seeing heavy non-renewal activity right now.

**Final expense — the soft opener:**
> "Hey [Name], this is Bill. Back in [month] you asked for info on final expense coverage. Just checking in to see if you got that handled, or if it's still open. No pressure at all — just closing the loop."

Works because seniors shut down on aggressive openers. They open up when they feel respected.

**Medicare — the AEP opener:**
> "Hey [Name], Bill with [Company]. You looked at Medicare plans back in [month]. With AEP starting [date], I wanted to see if you'd want me to walk you through your options for this year's enrollment. We'd do a Scope of Appointment first and then review."

Works because it's timely, useful, and CMS-compliant.

## Email 4 — Day 7 (nurture)

**Subject:** The renewal-cycle nurture most agents miss

**Preheader:** Insurance has a recurring event calendar. Use it.

---

{{firstName}},

Every insurance product has a renewal cycle. Every renewal cycle is a re-buying opportunity. The aged-lead operators who get rich understand this — and the ones who don't, don't.

**After Day 14, 85%+ of your aged leads won't have closed.** That's normal. Your job is to stay professionally present until their renewal cycle, life event, or carrier behavior creates a new window.

**Nurture triggers by line:**

**Auto** — target renewal windows (most carriers: 6 or 12 months from original policy date). Rate increases are visible in the data. Reach out 30 days before renewal.

**Home** — reach out at the start of hurricane/wildfire/winter-storm season depending on geography. Premium-adjustment letters typically land 60–90 days before renewal.

**Term life** — no annual renewal, but life events drive re-shopping. New babies, home purchases, kid aging out of policy coverage. Birthday-driven reach-outs work.

**Final expense** — anniversary touches only, very soft. Never urgency language.

**Medicare** — AEP (Oct 15–Dec 7). This is your annual fire-drill. Every Cold Medicare lead gets a touch in September to set up an AEP review.

**Health (ACA)** — OEP (Nov 1–Jan 15) + SEP triggers (job change, move, income change).

**The three-bucket nurture system** is in the playbook. Map every non-closed lead into Warm/Cold/Dormant by Day 14. Then let the calendar run.

**Insurance-specific content library:**
- Rate trend updates (quarterly for auto and home)
- Renewal reminders (timed to policy date)
- Seasonal content (hurricane prep, tax time, AEP)
- Sub-vertical-specific education (simplified vs traditional underwriting, MAPD vs Medigap, etc.)

**Your move this week:**

1. Open Worksheet 6 — map your current database into the three buckets.
2. Build your sub-vertical-specific nurture content library (6+ pieces).
3. Set calendar triggers tied to renewal cycles, AEP, and OEP for every line you sell.

## Email 5 — Day 10 (compliance + final CTA)

**Subject:** The compliance rules that will save your career

**Preheader:** State DOI, Medicare CMS, final expense senior rules.

---

{{firstName}},

Last email. Insurance compliance is layered — federal TCPA on the outreach side, state DOI licensing on the sales side, CMS for Medicare, UDAP-style for senior products. Mess up any layer and your career is done.

**The core non-negotiables:**

1. **DNC scrub every list, every time.** Federal + state DNCs (FL, OK, WA, MD among the strictest). Texas updated its rules in August 2025 — check current state.

2. **State DOI licensing before calling.** You cannot sell a policy in a state where you're not licensed. Aged lead files are multi-state; check before you dial.

3. **Run Mode A on inherited aged-lead consent.** Manual dial, plain-text email, voicemail. Escalate to SMS and automation only after you've captured fresh PEWC directly from the prospect (Fresh-Consent Ladder, Part IV of playbook).

4. **On the 1:1 rule (as of 2026):** vacated by the 11th Circuit Jan 2025, repealed by the FCC Aug 2025. PEWC is the live standard. The safest posture is still to own your consent — earn it fresh rather than inherit it.

5. **Medicare — separate workflow.** Scope of Appointment forms, recorded telephonic enrollment, CMS disclaimers. Not optional, heavily audited. If you sell Medicare and don't have an SOA process, stop outreach until you do.

6. **Final expense — UDAP hygiene.** No implied urgency. No government-endorsement implications. Calm, accurate, respectful. Senior-targeting states like NY, CA, MA have extra protections.

7. **Call time windows.** 8 AM–9 PM federal, 8 PM cap in FL, OK, WA, MD, and others. FL/OK cap at 3 calls/texts per day per subject.

**When you have a real compliance question, the firm I trust is [Henson Legal](https://www.henson-legal.com/).** For Medicare-specific questions, also engage a CMS-literate attorney — the Medicare Marketing Guidelines refresh annually and the details matter.

**Your move this week:**
