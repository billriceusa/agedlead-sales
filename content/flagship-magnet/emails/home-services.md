## Email 2 — Day 2 (setup + capacity)

**Subject:** Lead volume = field-crew bookings — size accordingly

**Preheader:** The tech stack comes second. Your estimator calendar comes first.

---

{{firstName}},

Home services is the one vertical where tech stack is the #2 question. #1 is estimator capacity.

If your lead volume produces 40 booked estimates a week and you only have 2 estimators, you have bottlenecked your operation before the first lead hits the CRM. Every unbooked estimate is a dead lead — they don't wait.

**Start with capacity math:**

- How many estimates can each field rep run per week? (typical: 8–15)
- How many reps do you have available?
- Your weekly appointment ceiling: reps × estimates/week

Then work backward:

- Weekly appointments ÷ appt-to-sold rate = weekly jobs sold
- Weekly jobs sold × ARPC = weekly revenue potential
- Weekly revenue potential ÷ 52 = annual revenue ceiling

**If your field capacity caps revenue below your business goals, hiring estimators comes before buying more leads.**

**Tech stack tiers for home services:**

**Setup A — No-tech (under 500 leads/mo):**
- VoIP + SMS tool + Google Sheet + Google Calendar for booking: ~$50/mo
- One tradeoff: your estimator's calendar is shared manually. At small volume, that's fine.

**Setup B — Light-tech (500–5,000 leads/mo):**
- CRM w/ scheduling integration (Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, or general like Close/GoHighLevel): $200–$600/mo
- Automated appointment reminders (text 24 hrs before, morning-of)
- Route optimization for field reps

**Setup C — Full-tech (5,000+ leads/mo):**
- Field-service platform (ServiceTitan, etc.) with CRM + dispatch + invoicing
- $500–$1,500+/mo per rep

**Home services-specific:** even in Setup A, invest in automated appointment reminders. No-show rate without reminders is 20–30%. With reminders, it drops to 5–10%. On a $4,500 ARPC and 30% close rate, every saved no-show is worth ~$500 to your top line.

**Your move this week:**

1. Worksheet 2 — pick your tier
2. Calculate your field-capacity ceiling (reps × estimates/week × appt-to-sold × ARPC × 52)
3. If lead volume would overwhelm capacity, adjust purchase plan

## Email 3 — Day 4 (outreach + two-step funnel)

**Subject:** The one rule of home-services outbound

**Preheader:** Never try to close on the call. Always book the estimate.

---

{{firstName}},

The biggest mistake new home-services operators make on aged leads: they try to close on the phone.

**You cannot sell a roof, a solar system, or a kitchen remodel over the phone.** Your prospect cannot make a $4K, $15K, or $25K decision based on a voice on the line. The goal of the call is **one thing only**: book the estimate.

Everything in the 14-day cadence, every script, every follow-up — all built around booking the in-home or virtual estimate. The close happens at the estimate, with your field rep, on the driveway.

**Opener — roofing example:**

> "Hey [Name], Bill with [Company]. A couple months back you looked at getting quotes on your roof — just checking in. Did you get it handled, or is your insurance still dragging on the claim?"

If "still dragging" or "haven't moved yet":

> "Totally get it. Can we get someone out there next week to give you a fresh look and a real number? Free estimate, 30 minutes, no pressure — worst case you get a second opinion on the claim."

**Book the appointment.** Don't explain more. Don't pitch. Get on the calendar.

**Sub-vertical script variants:**

**Solar — payback-period hook:**
> "Utility rates are up about [%] since you last looked. Payback period has shortened for most folks. Want to get someone out to run fresh numbers on your address?"

**Windows — financing hook:**
> "Running a [specific financing offer] through [end date]. Can we get you on the schedule for a free estimate before slots fill up?"

**HVAC — pre-season urgency:**
> "Heading into [season], install slots fill up. Since you were looking back in [month], worth getting you on the schedule now if you're still interested?"

**Remodel — soft, patient:**
> "Kitchen/bath projects are long cycles — totally get if you're still in planning mode. When you want a fresh look, I can get you on the estimate calendar."

**Your move this week:**

1. Worksheet 5 — customize your opener and book-the-estimate language
2. Rehearse until you can deliver without thinking about it
3. Load the 14-day cadence (Worksheet 6)

**The neighborhood tactic.** When you book a job on a street, every aged lead in that zip code gets a soft touch: "We're doing [project] two blocks from you — want me to have the rep swing by while they're in the neighborhood?" Highest-converting outreach play in home services.

## Email 4 — Day 7 (nurture + seasonal plays)

**Subject:** Your aged leads are next spring's pipeline

**Preheader:** Home services has the best nurture ROI in the industry — if you run it.

---

{{firstName}},

After Day 14 of the opener cadence, 80–90% of your aged home-services leads won't have booked an estimate. This is normal. It's also where the long-term money hides.

**Home services nurture ROI is extraordinary.** Projects have long decision cycles. Seasonal cycles. Life-event triggers (moving, babies, aging parents, insurance claims). Leads you don't close in the first 14 days routinely convert 6–18 months later when the right trigger fires.

**The three-bucket system:**

- **Warm:** responded to opener but didn't book. 2-week cadence for 3 months.
- **Cold:** didn't respond, didn't opt out. Touches at Month 1, 3, 6, 9, 12.
- **Dormant:** 12+ months silent. Monthly newsletter only.

**Home-services-specific nurture triggers:**

- **Seasonal.** Spring roofing. Summer HVAC. Fall windows. Winter pest prevention.
- **Storms / weather events.** When a hailstorm hits a zip, every aged roofing lead in that zip gets a touch (check state storm-chaser moratorium rules first).
- **Utility rate moves.** Solar leads get a nurture trigger when their utility raises rates.
- **Financing shifts.** When interest rates drop or a new promo launches, every aged remodel/window/solar lead gets an update.
- **Neighborhood proof.** When you complete a job on a street, every aged lead within a half-mile radius gets a "we're in your neighborhood" touch.

The neighborhood-proof play is unique to home services. **Maintain a zip-keyed nurture database** and automate this touch. Operators who do this get 15–25% lift in nurture-bucket conversion.

**Content library for home services:**

- Seasonal maintenance checklists
- Material-cost trend updates (lumber, steel, roofing materials, panel prices)
- Financing market updates
- Storm response and insurance-claim tips
- Neighborhood project highlights

**Your move this week:**

## Email 5 — Day 10 (compliance + final CTA)

**Subject:** The home-services compliance rules that matter

**Preheader:** TCPA, storm moratoriums, state contractor rules, financing disclosures.

---

{{firstName}},

Last email of the course. Home services compliance is specific — different from financial services in important ways.

**The core non-negotiables:**

1. **DNC scrub every list, every time.** Federal + state. Home services is heavily targeted by TCPA plaintiff firms — assume every number could be a plaintiff.

2. **Post-storm moratoriums.** Several states (FL, TX, LA, others) restrict roofing and related solicitation for a window after declared disasters. Before running aged outreach in a state post-storm, verify you're not in a moratorium.

3. **State contractor-board rules.** Many states (CA, TX, FL, NY) have specific rules on contractor solicitation, "free inspection" language, and gift-for-appointment restrictions.

4. **"Free estimate" not "free inspection."** The word "inspection" can trigger regulated-solicitation requirements in some states. "Estimate" is safer.

5. **Financing disclosures.** If you promote "0% financing" or any rate/term, you may trigger Regulation Z disclosures. Work with your financing partner on compliant script language.

6. **Run Mode A on inherited aged-lead consent.** Manual dial, plain-text email. SMS only after you've captured fresh PEWC via the Fresh-Consent Ladder (playbook Part IV). **No predictive/parallel dialing. No ringless voicemail** — the FCC ruled in 2022 that RVM to cell phones requires consent.

7. **On the 1:1 rule (2026):** vacated by the 11th Circuit and repealed by the FCC. PEWC is the live standard. Safest posture: earn fresh consent.

8. **Call time windows.** 8 AM–9 PM federal, 8 PM cap in FL, OK, WA, MD. FL/OK cap 3 calls/texts per day per subject.

9. **D2D overlap.** Local permits, "no soliciting" signs, federal 3-day cooling-off rule on in-home sales $25+.

**When you have a compliance question, the firm I trust is [Henson Legal](https://www.henson-legal.com/).** For state contractor-board questions, pair with a licensed contractor attorney in each state you operate.
