# Home Services Overlay — Aged Home Services Leads

*Companion to The Aged Lead Operator's System. Read the master playbook first.*

This overlay covers aged leads in home services: roofing, solar, windows, HVAC, kitchen/bath remodel, pest control, foundation, and related trades. The dynamics are different from financial-services leads — the prospect usually has a physical asset (the house) with a problem that doesn't go away on its own.

> **Compliance mode reminder.** Default to Mode A for inherited aged-lead consent: manual dial, plain-text email, fresh-consent ladder. Home services has heavy TCPA plaintiff-firm activity, plus state-specific storm-moratorium and contractor-solicitation rules. Until your consent workflow is tight, **stay in Mode A.** Use Fresh-Consent Ladder plays to graduate specific prospects to Mode B.
>
> **Recommended counsel:** [Henson Legal](https://www.henson-legal.com/) for TCPA and state solicitation questions. Pair with a state-licensed contractor-board attorney if you operate in a state with strict contractor marketing rules.

**Two-step funnel.** Home services is the one vertical where contact-to-close is almost always a two-step funnel: contact → appointment (in-home or virtual estimate) → sold. Your scripts need to optimize for *booking the estimate*, not closing on the call.

**Break-even math example — roofing.** $4,500 ARPC, 15% contact rate, 20% contact-to-appointment, 30% appointment-to-sold, 4× target return:

```
Contact-to-sold = 20% × 30% = 6%
Max CPL = $4,500 × 0.25 × 0.15 × 0.06 = $10.13
```

Home services can tolerate higher CPLs than most verticals because ARPCs are higher and the estimate step produces strong closing rates in the hands of a good field rep.

## Sub-vertical playbooks

### Roofing

**Prospect intent:** storm damage, age replacement, insurance claim support.

**The 3-month-later pitch:** roofing leads often shopped *because* a storm rolled through. 3 months later, many have discovered the damage was worse than they thought, or their insurance claim stalled. Open with: "Did you end up getting the roof handled, or is your insurance still dragging?"

**Seasonal note:** roofing has strong seasonality. Spring/early summer is peak. Aged leads from fall are often your best buy — they didn't close before winter, and now spring is their window.

**Supplement vs. replace.** Many aged roofing leads are repair/supplement prospects (roof is old but not ready to replace). Run a longer nurture on these — their replacement timer is 1–3 years out.

### Solar

**Prospect intent:** utility bill reduction, tax credit, eco-values, home resale value.

**The 3-month-later pitch:** solar economics have shifted rapidly as utility rates, panel prices, and the ITC have all moved. A prospect who shopped 3+ months ago got one set of numbers; today's numbers are often meaningfully different. Open with: "Have you seen what your utility bill's done since you looked at solar last?"

**Sub-segments:**
- High utility-cost states (CA, HI, NY, NJ, MA) — strongest economics
- Recently-moved homeowners — have cleared the initial "settle in" phase and are now ready for big projects
- Backyard battery / backup shoppers — different product, different close

**Lead-quality caution.** Solar has the widest variance in aged-lead quality. Cheaper solar lead files are often heavily over-sold and have low contact rates. Buy smaller batches first; measure contact rate; size up only if it beats 10%.

### Windows

**Prospect intent:** energy efficiency, aesthetic upgrade, noise, storm prep.

**The 3-month-later pitch:** windows are often a "someday" project that gets deferred for budget reasons. Financing and seasonal promos are your pitch. "Interest rates on financing moved — worth revisiting what you looked at back in [month]."

**Whole-home vs. partial.** Most aged window leads are partial-replacement shoppers (3–5 windows). The upsell to whole-home is where ARPC doubles. Your estimate script should diagnose whole-home need.

### HVAC (install)

**Prospect intent:** replacement urgency (current system failing), energy-efficiency upgrade, home sale prep.

**The 3-month-later pitch:** HVAC often doesn't wait. If a prospect shopped for a new system 3+ months ago and didn't buy, either they got it handled or their current system is limping along. Direct probe: "Did you end up replacing the system, or is it still the original?"

**Seasonal note:** HVAC is hottest during the vertical's stress season (summer for AC, winter for heat). An aged lead from last summer shopping AC is prime pre-season bait heading into this summer.

### Kitchen / bath remodel

**Prospect intent:** lifestyle upgrade, home sale prep, long-planned project.

**The 3-month-later pitch:** remodels are the longest-cycle home-services project. Aged leads routinely convert 6–18 months after their original inquiry. This sub-vertical has the highest *nurture* value and the lowest *opener* urgency. Plan accordingly — don't burn budget trying to close on Day 1.

**Sub-segments:**
- Kitchen-only (faster decision, lower ARPC)
- Whole-house / addition (longer cycle, higher ARPC)
- Accessibility remodels (aging-in-place, often insurance-funded)

### Pest control

**Prospect intent:** acute pest issue, preventive / seasonal, termite inspection.

**The 3-month-later pitch:** pest problems don't age gracefully. A prospect who shopped pest control 3 months ago and didn't buy either (a) DIY'd it and now has a worse problem, or (b) used a competitor who isn't keeping up. Fast, direct opener: "Did the pest issue get handled, or is it still going?"

This sub-vertical has the highest contact rate and best close rate in the overlay — because the problem doesn't wait.

### Foundation

**Prospect intent:** acute structural concern, pre-sale repair, insurance-covered damage.

**The 3-month-later pitch:** foundation issues get worse over time. A prospect who got quotes 3 months ago and hasn't moved is probably (a) trying to decide between providers, (b) waiting on insurance, or (c) procrastinating on bad news. Direct, empathetic script works: "I know foundation stuff is nobody's favorite project — did you end up getting it started, or is it still on the list?"

## Home-services-specific scripts

### Script HA1 — First-touch call (roofing)

> "Hey [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Company]. A couple months back you looked at getting quotes on your roof — just checking in to see if you got it handled, or if your insurance is still dragging on the claim."

### Script HA2 — First-touch call (solar)

> "Hey [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Company]. You looked at solar quotes back in [month] — what happened with that? Did you end up pulling the trigger, or is it still on the list?"

If still exploring: "Numbers have moved a lot since you last looked — utility rates are up about [current % vs shop date], which changes the payback period meaningfully. Want me to run fresh numbers on your address real quick?"

### Script HA3 — First-touch call (windows, financing hook)

> "Hey [Name], [Your Name] with [Company]. You got window quotes back in [month] — we're running some financing specials heading into [season], and I wanted to see if windows are still on your list before we run out of slots for [season]."

The financing / slot-scarcity hook works well in home services because it's true — most contractors legitimately have seasonal schedules and financing terms that move.

### Script HA4 — First-touch call (HVAC, pre-season)

> "Hey [Name], [Your Name] with [Company]. You looked at HVAC replacement back in [month] — with [season] coming up, I wanted to see if you got the system replaced or if you're still limping along with the old one."

### Script HA5 — First-touch call (kitchen/bath)

> "Hey [Name], [Your Name] with [Company]. You were looking at kitchen/bath remodel back in [month] — long project cycle on those, totally get it. Just wanted to check in and see where you landed — are you still in planning mode, or did you move forward with someone?"

Soft tone. Remodel prospects are almost always deliberating, and an aggressive opener will close the door.

### Script HA6 — Voicemail (generic home services)

> "Hey [Name] — [Your Name] with [Company]. Calling about the [roofing / solar / windows / HVAC / etc.] question you had back in [month]. Wanted to see if you got it handled or if you still need a fresh look. Give me a ring at [number] or I'll try you in a couple days."

### Home services objection library

**"I already hired someone."** → "Nice — who'd you end up going with? Any hiccups?" Two purposes: competitive intel, and if the project had problems, you're the rescue call. Add to Cold nurture with a 6-month refresh touch.

**"I decided not to do it."** → "Fair — what made you pull back? Was it the quote, timing, or just the project itself?" Diagnose. If it was quote-only, you have a re-engagement opportunity once financing/pricing shifts.

**"Send me a quote."** → "I'd love to, but for [roofing/solar/windows/HVAC] it's really hard to give you a useful number without putting eyes on the house. Can we get 30 minutes on the calendar for an estimate? Free, no pressure, and you get a real number instead of a guess."

**"Just looking, not ready to buy."** → "Totally fine — a lot of our best customers started in just-looking mode. When you're thinking of pulling the trigger — this year, next year, further out?" Map their timeline. That's your nurture anchor.

## Compliance notes specific to home services

### TCPA — no different, but enforcement is aggressive

Home services is one of the most-TCPA-targeted verticals for both consumer complaints and plaintiff's-firm surveillance. Treat every number as if a TCPA plaintiff might be on the other end.

- Scrub every number against federal DNC. Every time. Scrub state DNC where applicable.
- Document your consent record per lead.
- SMS 10DLC registration — required.
- **No predictive or parallel dialing on inherited aged-lead consent.** Preview dial only.
- **No ringless voicemail.** FCC ruled November 2022 that RVM to wireless is a "call" under TCPA.
- On the 1:1 rule: the FCC's proposed 1-to-1 consent rule was vacated by the 11th Circuit (Jan 2025) and repealed by the FCC (Aug 2025). PEWC is the live standard. Inherited consent workable for manual outreach; weak for automation — use Fresh-Consent Ladder to escalate.

### Home-services-specific fresh-consent plays

Home services has the strongest fresh-consent capture on the front end because the prospect already wants a physical outcome — they just need a reason to raise their hand:

1. **Free estimate booking form** — the highest-converting fresh-consent moment. Gate every estimate booking behind a form with an explicit PEWC checkbox. Almost everyone who gets to the form wants the estimate and will check the box.
2. **Storm-damage photo upload** — for roofing especially. A landing page that lets the prospect upload photos of their roof for a preliminary evaluation. Form captures fresh PEWC + produces a visual lead asset.
3. **Neighborhood-project text opt-in** — "Text [zip code] to [number] to hear when we're in your neighborhood" — great local, geo-targeted fresh consent.
4. **Financing pre-qualification** — "Text QUAL to [number] for a soft-pull pre-qualification on financing for your project." Hot-intent, fresh PEWC capture.
5. **Seasonal maintenance reminder opt-in** — "Text REMIND to [number] for quarterly home-maintenance reminders." Low-intent, but long-tail nurture asset.

### State-specific contractor / solicitation rules

Many states have specific rules on home-services solicitation:

- **California** — CSLB rules on contractor marketing, plus specific solar solicitation requirements.
- **Florida** — post-storm moratorium rules prohibit unsolicited contact after declared disasters.
- **Texas** — specific rules on roofing solicitation.
- **Many states** — "gift for appointment" restrictions limit how you can incentivize estimate bookings.

Know your states. Your sales rep who promises a $100 gift card for scheduling an appointment may be violating state solicitation law without knowing it.

### Storm-chasing restrictions

Post-storm, several states temporarily restrict solicitation for roofing and related trades. The purpose is consumer protection — to prevent fly-by-night storm chasers. If you're running aged leads in a state after a major weather event, pause the outreach and verify you're not in a moratorium window.

### "Free inspection" language

Offering a "free inspection" sounds benign, but some state contractor boards treat it as a regulated solicitation. Language like "free estimate" is generally safer than "free inspection" because it implies a sales-qualification interaction rather than a diagnostic claim.

### Financing disclosures

If you promote financing in your outreach (SMS, email, calls), you may trigger Regulation Z disclosure requirements (APR, terms, fees). "0% financing" mentioned on a cold call without the full disclosure behind it is a compliance risk. Work with your financing partner to build compliant script language.

### Door-to-door overlap

Home-services teams often pair aged-lead outreach with door-to-door follow-up on unreached prospects. D2D has its own compliance layer:
- Local permits required in many municipalities
- "No soliciting" signs must be honored
- Federal cooling-off rule (3-day right to cancel on in-home sales over $25)

## Home-services-specific nurture content

Build your nurture library around the project lifecycle:

**Pre-project:**
- Seasonal home-maintenance checklists
- Financing market updates
- Material-cost trend updates (lumber, steel, copper, panel prices)
- "What to know before you hire" buyer's guides

**Post-close (customer) but aged-lead-style:**
- Maintenance reminders (roofing inspection, HVAC service, window caulking)
- Warranty check-ins
- Upsell nurture for related projects

**Event-triggered:**
- Post-storm outreach (weather-appropriate, and compliant with state rules)
- Post-utility-rate-change (solar, windows)
- Post-neighborhood-activity (we did X houses on your street — want us to look at yours?)

### The "neighborhood" nurture angle

Home services gets a unique nurture angle that other verticals don't: local proof. Map your aged leads by zip. When you book a job in a zip code, send a soft touch to every aged lead in that zip: "Hey, we're doing a [project] around the corner from you at [street name] — want me to swing by while we're in the neighborhood and give you a fresh estimate?" This is one of the highest-ROI nurture plays in home services.
