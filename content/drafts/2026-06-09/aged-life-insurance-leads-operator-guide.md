# Aged Life Insurance Leads: The Operator's System for Contact, Cadence, and Close (2026)

Every agent who moves to aged leads eventually asks the same question: "If I can buy 200 aged term life leads for what one real-time lead costs, why isn't everyone doing this?"

The math is obviously compelling. What's less obvious is that the math only works if you run a system against those leads — not just a dialer. Agents who buy 500 aged life insurance leads with no cadence plan churn through them in a week, call nobody twice, and conclude that aged leads don't work. Agents running an actual contact-and-nurture sequence on the same 500 records are pulling 5–8% close rates and a cost per closing under $150.

This is a piece about how the second group operates.

## The unit economics, before anything else

Aged life insurance leads in the 15–85 day bracket run $0.50–$3.00 per record depending on age, quantity, and vertical sub-type (term vs. final expense vs. IUL). Real-time, exclusive life insurance leads from premium platforms run $20–$50 per record.

Let's run Agent A vs. Agent B on a $1,500 prospecting budget.

**Agent A buys real-time exclusive leads.**
- Budget: $1,500
- Cost per lead: $35
- Leads purchased: 42
- Expected contact rate on exclusive fresh leads: ~40%
- Contacts made: 17
- Close rate: 18%
- Closings: 3
- Average commission (term life): $400
- Revenue: $1,200
- Net after lead cost: −$300

**Agent B buys aged leads (45–85 day bracket).**
- Budget: $1,500
- Cost per lead: $1.50
- Leads purchased: 1,000
- Expected contact rate with a 10-day multi-touch cadence: 12–15%
- Contacts made: 120–150
- Close rate on contacts: 5%
- Closings: 6–7
- Average commission (term life): $400
- Revenue: $2,400–$2,800
- Net after lead cost: $900–$1,300

Read those numbers again. Same $1,500. Same time period. Agent A is underwater; Agent B nets $900+. Agent A's close rate looks better on paper — 18% vs. 5% — but Agent B's *cost per closing* is roughly one-tenth of Agent A's.

The math doesn't guarantee results. What it guarantees is that if you can build contact rate and hold a reasonable close rate, the economics are overwhelmingly in your favor. The question is: how do you build contact rate on leads that multiple agents have already touched?

## Why aged life insurance leads are actually a different product

A real-time life insurance lead submitted yesterday has been called by 3–5 agents in the past hour. The prospect is in call-deflection mode. They may close with whoever finds them at the right moment, but they've already been conditioned to screen calls from unknown numbers.

An aged lead that's 60 days old has been called a handful of times in the first week after submission, then dropped entirely when those agents moved on. The prospect has had 7 weeks of silence. They're not in call-deflection mode anymore — they're back to baseline.

That's not a consolation prize. That's a different conversation. You're not the fifth agent calling this week; you're the one calling after everyone else gave up.

The conversion model is different, too. Life insurance requires more education, more touchpoints, and a longer decision window than auto or home insurance. Aged leads are a natural match for that sales cycle — they're not expecting to close on the first call, and neither should you.

## Lead age segmentation: work the brackets differently

Not all aged life leads are the same. The 15–45 day bracket, the 45–86 day bracket, and the 86–365 day bracket each require a different approach.

**15–45 days.** These leads were recently in-market and had an intent signal. They're the most expensive aged tier for a reason. Your opener is direct: they submitted a form recently, you're following up, you have information that might help. Speed-to-lead still matters in this bracket — don't sit on a fresh batch for three days before starting your cadence.

**45–86 days.** The sweet spot for volume buying. Enough time has passed that the flood of agents has dried up, but the underlying need that drove the form submission is still real. The prospect may have gotten a policy elsewhere in those 45 days, but statistically most haven't. Your opener is warmer: you're following up on a quote request from a couple months back, you understand they may have found coverage, but wanted to check in.

**86–365 days.** Bulk economics play. At $0.25–$0.50 per record, you can run high-volume outreach campaigns with email as the primary channel and phone as the secondary. Contact rates drop, but at $0.40 per lead you can afford a 4% contact rate and still produce a reasonable pipeline. This bracket is best for operators who have automated email sequences running and aren't dialing manually.

The strategic move is to work all three tiers in your pipeline simultaneously with different cadences and different primary channels.

## The 10-day contact cadence

This is the piece most operators skip. They buy leads, make one or two calls, don't connect, and move on. The data on aged lead contact rates consistently shows that most connections happen between touch 3 and touch 6 — not on the first attempt.

Here's a 10-day cadence that works for the 15–85 day bracket. Adapt the timing to your own call windows; the sequence matters more than the exact hours.

**Day 1 — Call + voicemail + email.**
Call during early evening (5:30–7:30 PM local time). If no answer, leave a brief voicemail: your name, that you're following up on a life insurance quote request, and a callback number. Send a plain-text email within the hour referencing the same.

**Day 2 — Email only.**
A short follow-up email. No call. This plants a second touchpoint without pressure. Keep it under 75 words.

**Day 3 — Morning call.**
Call between 9–11 AM local. No voicemail this time — voicemails on back-to-back days train people to screen. If you connect, this is your first real conversation. If not, move on.

**Day 5 — Call + voicemail.**
A different message in this voicemail. Reference that life circumstances change — you wanted to make sure they had the information they requested.

**Day 7 — Email.**
A value-add email: a brief explainer of the difference between term and whole life, or a 3-question exercise to estimate coverage needs. Position yourself as the resource, not the pitch.

**Day 9 — Call.**
Final call attempt this round. If you reach them, great. If not, no voicemail — just log the attempt.

**Day 10 — Long-tail sequence email.**
A brief email that says you're moving them to a lower-cadence follow-up and they should reach out when the timing is right. This email closes the active sequence but keeps the door open.

Six contact attempts across 10 days. Mix of calls, voicemails, and emails. This is what produces 12–15% contact rates on aged life insurance leads when a single-call approach produces 3–5%.

## Compliance in 2026: what you're actually allowed to do

The regulatory landscape shifted materially in 2025. Here's the current state.

The FCC's 1:1 consent rule — which would have required a separate written consent for each lead buyer to contact a consumer — was vacated by the Eleventh Circuit in January 2025 and repealed in August 2025. As of now, leads collected under a multi-buyer consent form (which is how most internet lead vendors, including Aged Lead Store, operate) remain legally workable.

Separately, the FCC's revocation-of-consent "all-at-once" rule was delayed to January 2027. For the next 18 months, you don't have to stop all contact when a prospect revokes consent on one channel — a revocation on SMS doesn't automatically kill your calling rights on that record. That changes in 2027.

State-level mini-TCPAs are active and getting stricter. Florida's FTSA, Oklahoma's OTSA, and Washington's HB 1497 all impose meaningful restrictions on auto-dialed and unsolicited SMS outreach. Maryland and Texas have similar frameworks in various stages. If you're dialing into those states, run the list through current state-level scrubs — not just federal DNC.

**The practical mode framework.**

Mode A (conservative): Manual dial only. Plain-text email. DNC-scrubbed. No SMS. This is the safest approach for inherited aged consent and works well for solo operators who have the time to dial manually.

Mode B (operator standard): DNC-scrubbed. Multi-channel including SMS and power dialer. This works when you're working leads from a vendor who provides TCPA-compliant consent documentation and you've confirmed the consent chain. This is the mode most high-volume operators run.

Mode C: Aggressive auto-dialing without DNC scrubs, relying on original consent language that may or may not cover current outreach. Mode C is not recommended by this site or by [Henson Legal](https://www.henson-legal.com/), who we refer for TCPA and state mini-TCPA counsel.

When in doubt, run Mode A on unfamiliar lead sources and graduate to Mode B once you've confirmed the consent documentation. The cost of conservative compliance is slower dialing. The cost of aggressive non-compliance is $500–$1,500 per violation in states like Florida.

## Scripts that open the conversation

Aged life insurance leads have been pitched before. Generic openers — "Hi, I'm following up on your life insurance inquiry" — get reflexive hang-ups. The best openers do three things: identify the original inquiry, acknowledge time has passed, and ask a question rather than launching a pitch.

**Opener for 45–85 day leads:**
> "Hi [first name], my name is [your name] with [agency]. You submitted a request for life insurance information back in [month] — I wanted to follow up and see if you're still looking or if you've already found coverage."

The second clause is important. Asking "have you already found coverage?" removes the pressure and often gets an honest answer. If they found coverage, you're done. If they haven't, you've opened a real conversation.

**For final expense leads specifically:**
The psychology is different. These prospects are often older and more deliberate. The opener acknowledges that:
> "Hi [first name], this is [name] from [agency]. I saw that you were looking into final expense coverage a couple months back. A lot of people take a little longer with this kind of decision, which makes sense — I wanted to check in and see if you had any questions I could help answer."

**For IUL/whole life leads:**
These prospects are often evaluating life insurance as a financial instrument, not just a protection product. Your opener should reflect that:
> "Hi [first name], I'm following up on a life insurance quote request you submitted — specifically around [term/whole life/IUL]. You may have had a chance to do more research since then — where are you in your thinking?"

Opening questions, not opening pitches. That's what converts aged life insurance leads.

## What to do with the 80% you don't close in 10 days

The contact cadence above should produce 12–15% contact rate. Of those contacts, a 5% close rate means roughly 6–8% of the original 1,000 leads become clients. What about the other 994?

They go into a long-tail email nurture sequence. This isn't aggressive follow-up — it's quarterly value-delivery content. Insurance news, a rate-change alert for their state, a short piece on the difference between coverage types. You're staying top of mind for the 15–20% of aged leads who will eventually convert when their circumstances change — a new job, a divorce, a new child, a coverage gap they just realized.

This is where the economics of aged leads compound over time. A real-time lead you don't close in the first week is a wasted asset. An aged lead you don't close in the first 10 days is a contact you maintain at near-zero incremental cost. The difference is that aged lead operators tend to think in terms of lead lifetime value, not lead-to-close rate.

## What to do Monday morning

If you want to run the system above on a real budget, here's the sequence:

- **Start with 250–500 records in the 45–85 day bracket.** Enough to learn the cadence without overextending.
- **Load into a CRM with task sequences.** Even a basic setup in HubSpot or Follow Up Boss will outperform manual tracking. Set follow-up tasks to fire on Day 1, 3, 5, 7, 9.
- **Scrub against DNC before you dial.** Every 31 days minimum, or before each new batch.
- **Run Mode A to start.** Manual dial, email-only outreach. If you're in a high-risk state (FL, OK, WA), confirm with [Henson Legal](https://www.henson-legal.com/) before moving to SMS.
- **Track contact rate and cost per contact by lead age bracket.** You'll quickly see which bracket pencils for your vertical and commission structure.
- **Build the long-tail list.** Everyone who picks up but doesn't close goes into a long-tail email sequence, not the trash.

The system compounds. The operator who's been running aged life insurance leads for 12 months has a long-tail nurture list of 800 prospects alongside their current active cadence. That's how you build a pipeline that doesn't reset to zero every quarter.
