# Aged Life Insurance Leads: Scripts and Working Strategies for FE, Term, and IUL Agents

There are three markets inside "aged life insurance leads," and they operate nothing alike.

Final expense leads come from a senior demographic where the underlying mortality concern that drove the original form-fill hasn't resolved — the prospect is still 70+ years old, still without burial coverage. Term leads are a mid-market volume play with a steeper intent-decay curve. IUL leads are consultative, high-commission, and the sub-vertical where aged inventory gives independent agents their strongest structural edge over large captive shops.

Most guides treat aged life insurance leads as a single category. They aren't. This guide covers how to buy, dial, and close in all three — including the scripts, cadences, and compliance posture specific to each.

## Why life insurance leads hold their value longer than other verticals

A life insurance lead at 60, 90, or 180 days is less compromised than a 60-day-old auto or mortgage lead — and the reason is structural.

In auto insurance, a consumer who submitted a rate comparison in January may have renewed their existing policy by March. The need resolved. In short-term mortgage, a purchase-intent consumer may have bought a home or stopped looking. Intent decays quickly when the trigger event resolves.

Life insurance is different. A 75-year-old who submitted a final expense form six months ago still hasn't solved the burial-coverage gap. A 45-year-old IUL inquiry from January is still building retirement wealth. The financial or protective goal that drove the original inquiry persists in a way auto and mortgage intent does not.

This holds especially for FE and IUL. Term life has a steeper decay curve — consumers do sometimes buy from another agent or defer indefinitely — but even aged term outperforms auto or mortgage at comparable age tiers.

## Final expense — the highest-volume, highest-compliance-exposure sub-vertical

Final expense is the most active aged life insurance sub-vertical by lead volume. It's also the one with the most compliance exposure, because the senior demographic triggers stricter protections under state mini-TCPAs.

### What FE leads look like at 30–90 days

A 30–90-day final expense lead is typically a 60–80-year-old who filled out an internet form or responded to a direct mail piece requesting burial insurance information. Real-time buyers hit them hard in the first week and most stop after two or three contact attempts. By day 30, competitive noise has cleared. The prospect hasn't bought a policy — they're still senior, still uncovered, and now not being actively worked by anyone else.

### FE lead types and aged-market pricing

Final expense has the broadest channel diversity of any life insurance sub-vertical:

| Type | Age tier | Pricing range |
|---|---|---|
| Internet form, shared | 31–85 days | $0.62–$1.88 |
| Internet form, shared | 86–180 days | $0.40–$0.80 |
| Direct mail response, exclusive | 31–85 days | $8–$25 |
| Live transfer, real-time | Real-time | $50–$80 |

The direct mail response tier is the one most agents underestimate. A consumer who responded to a physical mail piece — returning a card or calling a number — took an offline action. That's a meaningfully different intent signal than an internet form, and experienced FE telesales operations see 3–4x the conversion rate on direct mail response leads versus internet-form aged leads at the same age tier.

### FE script — Mode A (TCPA-conservative)

Mode A assumes manual dialing only, no automated dialer or prerecorded messages. This is the appropriate posture for 86+-day-old FE inventory and for calling in FL, OK, and WA, where state mini-TCPAs impose stricter rules on autodialers targeting seniors.

**First dial:**
> "Hi, [first name]? This is [your name] with [agency name]. You had reached out a while back looking for some information about final expense insurance — is that something you're still exploring? No pressure at all, I just wanted to make sure you got a chance to talk to someone."

**If they say "I don't remember" or "Who are you again?":**
> "Totally fair — it may have been a while back. What you filled out was a form looking for information about coverage that would take care of final expenses — funeral costs, that kind of thing. Are you still thinking about that, or did you get something in place already?"

**If they say "I already bought something":**
> "That's great — glad you got it handled. Just out of curiosity, when did you put that in place? [If within 6 months: great, enjoy the coverage. If more than 6 months ago: you might want to pull it out and look at the premium — some of those policies step up in cost after the first year.]"

The Mode A approach is slower per call but lower-risk in high-exposure states and on older inventory.

### FE script — Mode B (operator standard)

Mode B uses a power or predictive dialer with real-time opt-out infrastructure. Acceptable for most 31–85-day aged internet-form FE inventory, but requires verification that the original form consent language is TCPA-compliant and that your DNC scrubbing is current. Ask your vendor for the original disclosure language before running Mode B on any new FE batch.

**Mode B opener:**
> "Hi, is [first name] available? [Pause.] Hi [first name], this is [your name] from [agency]. You had filled out some information about final expense insurance — are you still looking for coverage on that, or did you get it taken care of?"

### FE dialing cadence — 21 days, 6 touches

| Day | Touch | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Call #1 + voicemail | Phone |
| 2 | Call #2 (no voicemail) | Phone |
| 4 | Email #1 (intro + educational) | Email |
| 7 | Call #3 + voicemail | Phone |
| 12 | Call #4 (no voicemail) | Phone |
| 21 | Call #5 + email #2 (final outreach) | Phone + Email |

Front-load the first seven days — most FE aged-lead contacts happen in days 1–7 or not at all. The [Outreach Cadence Planner](/calculators/outreach-cadence-planner) will model total touches against your call capacity if you're running a larger FE batch.

## Term life — the gateway sub-vertical

Term life aged leads have a steeper intent-decay curve than FE or IUL. A 30-year-old who submitted a term quote form in January may have resolved their need by April — bought from another agent, deferred, or decided they don't currently need it. That decay is more pronounced in term than in either other life sub-vertical.

That doesn't make aged term leads unworkable. It changes the strategy.

### The right use case for aged term leads

Aged term works best as the gateway product for a multi-product life insurance practice. The term conversation opens the relationship. The cross-sell to whole life, IUL, or final expense (for older prospects) is where the margin lives.

Solo term agents running a high-volume, single-product operation can make aged term work at $5–$15 per record — but only with a tight 14-day cadence and only if the math supports a 1–1.5% close rate on contacts. The full CPA analysis is in the [life insurance leads cost guide](/blog/life-insurance-leads-cost-2026).

### Term script — Mode B (standard opener)

> "Hey [first name], this is [your name] from [agency]. I'm calling about a term life insurance quote you had requested — I know it's been a while. Has anything changed since then? Did you ever get that taken care of?"

The key: don't over-explain the delay. One acknowledging line ("I know it's been a while") handles the objection proactively and lets you get to qualification fast. Aged term prospects who are still in-market respond to directness.

### Term dialing cadence — 14 days, 5 touches

| Day | Touch | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Call #1 + voicemail | Phone |
| 2 | Call #2 (no voicemail) | Phone |
| 5 | Email #1 | Email |
| 9 | Call #3 + voicemail | Phone |
| 14 | Call #4 + email #2 (final) | Phone + Email |

Shorter cadence than FE because term intent decays faster. Prospects who haven't responded by day 14 are unlikely to convert from this batch.

## IUL — the highest-upside aged sub-vertical for independent agents

Indexed universal life is where aged leads give independent agents their strongest competitive edge over large captive operations — and where the math is most favorable.

Here's the structural reason: real-time IUL leads at $40–$75 per record get worked immediately by large shops with auto-dialers, dedicated speed-to-lead teams, and 24/7 inbound coverage. An independent producer or small agency can't win that arms race on real-time inventory. But aged IUL leads at $2–$6 per record have been abandoned by those same shops after one or two contact attempts. At day 30–60, the competition is near zero.

And IUL prospects often benefit from time. The product is complex — cash value accumulation, index participation, tax-advantaged growth, death benefit — and most consumers aren't ready to buy on a first call. By the time you reach them at day 30–60, they've had space to research, reflect on retirement goals, and digest the concept. That makes them more receptive to a consultative conversation, not less.

### IUL script — Mode A (first dial)

> "Hi [first name], this is [your name] with [agency]. You had looked into indexed universal life insurance a while back — I wanted to reach out to see if you're still thinking about that. I know these conversations usually take a few calls, not just one. Do you have a few minutes?"

The IUL opener should signal patience and consultative intent from the first sentence. IUL buyers are often also speaking with financial planners; a high-pressure pitch signals you're the wrong fit.

**If they ask "what's IUL again?":**
> "Completely fair — it's one of the more complex life insurance products. The short version is that it's permanent life insurance with a cash value component that grows based on a stock market index, with a floor so you can't lose the cash value in a down market. Most people who look at it are thinking about tax-advantaged retirement income. Does that ring a bell from when you were researching it?"

### IUL dialing cadence — 30 days, 7 touches

| Day | Touch | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Call #1 + voicemail | Phone |
| 3 | Email #1 (educational — what IUL is and how it works) | Email |
| 7 | Call #2 | Phone |
| 12 | Email #2 (case study or worked example) | Email |
| 17 | Call #3 + voicemail | Phone |
| 24 | Call #4 | Phone |
| 30 | Email #3 (final outreach + calendar booking link) | Email |

Longer cadence than FE or term because IUL requires multiple educational touchpoints before a prospect is ready to move to a proposal conversation. Use the email touches to deliver real value — a worked example of how a $500/month IUL policy accumulates cash value over 20 years outperforms any pitch in moving a prospect toward a meeting.

## Compliance — TCPA and the senior demographic

Final expense is the highest-compliance-risk sub-vertical because you're calling seniors. That matters more in 2026 than it did three years ago.

**The current regulatory state:**

- The FCC's 1:1 consent rule was vacated in January 2025 and repealed in August 2025. Aged internet-form leads with valid original consent remain usable under the pre-1:1 TCPA framework — this did not retroactively invalidate aged inventory.
- The FCC's revocation rule (consumers can revoke consent through "any reasonable means") has a delayed compliance deadline of January 2027. Build opt-out infrastructure for text and email revocation now — don't wait for the deadline.
- State mini-TCPAs: Florida's FTSA, Oklahoma's OTSA, and Washington's HB 1497 impose stricter restrictions on automated calls and texts to residents, with particular sensitivity to the senior demographic. If your FE call volume includes FL or OK, consult with [Henson Legal](https://www.henson-legal.com/) before running a predictive dialer on that inventory.

**Mode A / Mode B / Mode C — the operating framework:**

- **Mode A (TCPA-conservative):** Manual dialing only, no prerecorded messages, no automated texts. The right posture for 86+-day-old inventory and for FE in high-exposure states.
- **Mode B (operator standard):** Power or predictive dialer with real-time opt-out infrastructure, verified original consent documentation. Acceptable for most 31–85-day internet-form life insurance inventory.
- **Mode C (not recommended):** Autodialers on unverified lists, prerecorded messages without confirmed consent. Not a mode this guide endorses.

## Buying aged life insurance leads — the practical guide

**Filter decisions before you buy:**

1. **Sub-vertical first.** Buy FE, term, and IUL separately. Each needs a different script, cadence, and compliance posture — mixing them in the same batch creates operational confusion.
2. **Age tier.** 31–85 days is the standard starting point. 86–180 days works for FE (the underlying need persists); it's lower-value for term (intent decays).
3. **Consumer age band.** FE: 55–80. IUL: 35–55. Term: 25–50.
4. **State.** Buy states where you're licensed. For FE, factor compliance posture by state before you dial.
5. **Starter volume.** A $500–$1,000 test — 300–600 FE internet-form records at standard pricing — is enough to run a full 21-day cadence and get meaningful conversion data before scaling.

**What to track:**

Track cost per contact, cost per appointment set, and cost per bound policy — separately by sub-vertical. Most aged life insurance campaigns that "don't work" have a script or cadence problem rather than a lead quality problem. The data will tell you which. ## What this means for you

Aged life insurance leads are not one market. Final expense, term, and IUL each have their own aging curve, their own optimal cadence, and their own compliance posture.

A few things to carry out of this guide:

- Life insurance leads hold their value at older age tiers better than any other vertical. The underlying mortality concern or wealth-building goal doesn't resolve on a 90-day timer the way auto or short-term mortgage intent does.
- FE is the highest-volume sub-vertical and the most compliance-sensitive. If you dial in FL, OK, or WA, treat state mini-TCPAs as your floor, not TCPA federal minimums.
- IUL aged leads are the single highest-upside inventory tier for independent agents. The competition at 30–90 days is near zero; the commission ceiling absorbs the lower-than-real-time conversion rate.
- Buy sub-verticals separately and run separate cadences. Mixing FE, term, and IUL in the same batch with the same script is the fastest way to underperform all three.
