# Aged Life Insurance Leads: The 2026 Agent's Guide to Pricing, Working, and Closing

There isn't one life insurance leads market. There are three — and buying or working them as if they're the same thing is the fastest way to lose money on any of them.

Term life, final expense, and IUL attract different consumers, carry different commission structures, and age differently on your dialer. A $2,000 budget deployed in aged term leads produces completely different economics than the same $2,000 in aged final expense or aged IUL. The right allocation depends on what you sell — not on which price tier looks cheapest on a spreadsheet.

This is the breakdown: what aged life insurance leads cost across all three sub-markets, what the math looks like at real numbers, and how to work them once you've bought them.

## Three markets, three different economics

**Term life** is a volume play. Premiums run $40-150 per month, FYC lands at $500-2,000 depending on face amount and carrier, and the buyer is typically 25-50 years old with a specific trigger — new mortgage, new child, employer plan gap. Contact rates are reasonable. Close rates are thin. The math works only at scale and only when you keep your cost per lead well under $10. Aged term is ideally positioned here: internet-form leads running $1.25-2.00 for 15-85 day inventory, dropping to $0.25-0.40 for 86-365 day leads.

**Final expense** is different. The buyer is 55-80 years old, often responding to a direct-mail card or searching online for burial and final cost coverage. Monthly premiums run $30-80; FYC lands between $500 and $1,500 depending on carrier and product. The sales cycle is shorter than term — this is a telesales product — and the emotional close is different from term. You're competing on trust and simplicity, not price. Aged final expense leads perform better than almost any other vertical on a sustained cadence because the underlying problem doesn't resolve on a 90-day timer. The consumer who was looking for FE coverage in January is still looking in June.

**IUL** is where the math gets interesting. Monthly premiums of $300-1,000+, FYC of $3,000-10,000+ per policy, and a consultative sales cycle that takes multiple calls over multiple weeks. Competition on real-time IUL leads is fierce — you're one of five agents calling the same prospect in the first 24 hours. Aged IUL inventory, at $2-6 per lead, is the most underpriced category in the life market. The FYC ceiling is high enough to absorb either aged or real-time costs; the competition differential on aged is enormous, and almost no one is working it systematically.

## The math — $2,000 deployed across three sub-verticals

The only metric that actually matters is cost per closing. Here's what a $2,000 budget looks like deployed three different ways.

**Term life — aged 31–85 day leads at $1.50**

$2,000 budget. 1,333 leads. 14-day touch cadence across 7 contact attempts.

Cumulative contact rate on a properly executed 7-touch sequence: 25–30%. At 28%: 373 conversations.

Aged term agents typically close 1.5–2.5% of contacts. At 2%: 7.5 closings. Average FYC: $800. Gross revenue: $6,000. Net after lead cost: $4,000.

Cost per closing: $267. Return on lead spend: 4x.

That math works. What doesn't work is 100 leads and two calls. The law of averages demands volume — you need at least 300–500 leads to produce a statistically meaningful conversion sample in term.

**Final expense — aged 15–85 day leads at $0.90 average**

$2,000 budget. ~2,222 leads. 10-day phone-primary cadence.

Aged FE internet-form leads in telesales produce 20–30% cumulative contact rates on a disciplined cadence. At 25%: 555 conversations.

Close rate on telesales FE contacts for agents with a practiced phone close: 3–5%. At 3.5%: 19.4 closings. Average FYC: $750. Gross revenue: $14,550.

Cost per closing: $103. Net after lead cost: $12,550.

This is why final expense is the volume-economics king in life insurance. The arithmetic compounds aggressively when contact rates and close rates hold — which they do for agents who can run a consistent phone-first cadence.

**IUL — aged 15–85 day leads at $4.00**

$2,000 budget. 500 leads. Multi-touch consultative cadence over 21 days.

Aged IUL contact rates are lower than FE: 20–25%, because these are higher-income, harder-to-reach prospects. At 22%: 110 conversations.

IUL close rate on aged consultative contacts: 1.5–3%. At 2%: 2.2 closings. Average FYC: $5,500. Gross revenue: $12,100.

Cost per closing: $909. Net after lead cost: $10,100.

The per-closing cost is eight times the FE example — and the net is still $10,100 on $2,000 spent. That's the IUL math. The FYC ceiling is high enough that even at a fraction of FE's conversion volume, the economics hold for agents with the consultative skill to close it.

## Why aged life leads don't decay like other verticals

In auto insurance, a 120-day-old lead may have already renewed. In purchase mortgage, the lead from six months ago has likely resolved the decision — bought the house or stopped looking.

Life insurance is structurally different. The underlying need doesn't expire on a 90-day timer.

A 62-year-old who submitted a final expense inquiry in February still has the same problem in June — no coverage, aging dependents, modest fixed income. A 41-year-old who requested an IUL quote in January still has the same mortgage, the same wealth-transfer goal, the same questions about tax-advantaged growth. They didn't buy from someone else. They were overwhelmed by agents who called twice and gave up, and then they went back to their lives.

That's the opening you have with aged life inventory.

The price discount on 86–365 day life leads is dramatic — $0.25-0.40 per lead for term, $0.50-2.00 for IUL. The conversion opportunity doesn't decay at anything close to the same rate as the price. The 180-day-old pool isn't full of people who solved their problem. It's full of people no one followed up with systematically.

## Working aged life insurance leads — the cadence that converts

Volume is necessary. It's not sufficient. Agents who buy 2,000 life leads, make two calls, and write the rest off are paying for a one-touch mailing list. Aged life leads compound over 14–21 days of consistent outreach.

The cadence that produces results:

- **Day 1, morning:** Phone call. Voicemail if no answer: "This is [name] following up on your life insurance inquiry. I have some options to show you — takes five minutes."
- **Day 1, evening:** Email: same message, shorter.
- **Day 3:** Second call, different time slot. No voicemail.
- **Day 5:** Third call. Voicemail with a hook: "Rates shifted for your age bracket this month — worth a quick conversation."
- **Day 8:** Email only — a short value piece.
- **Day 12:** Final call. If still no contact: close-out email ("I'm closing your file — if timing changes, here's how to reach me").
- **Day 14–21:** Move FE and IUL leads to low-frequency nurture — one touch per month for 90 days. These buyers are still in the market.

For final expense specifically: phone is the primary channel. Seniors in the 60–75 demographic respond poorly to text outreach from unknown numbers, and Florida's FTSA creates meaningful legal exposure for text campaigns targeting Florida residents. Call during business hours — 9am to 5pm local time.

The script opening that outperforms the alternatives: "You requested a life insurance quote [timeframe] ago. I wanted to follow up before you made a final decision." That framing is honest, non-aggressive, and positions you as someone with a solution rather than someone working a cold list.

## Final expense — the direct mail aged tier

There's a sub-segment in final expense that doesn't get discussed enough: aged direct-mail response leads.

An internet-form lead is a consumer who clicked an ad and filled out a form — low friction, impulse behavior. A direct-mail response lead took a physical card, filled it out, and mailed it back. That's a different buyer. Higher intent, lower competition, longer consideration cycle. The conversion rate difference is real — DM response contacts in FE telesales close at 6–10% for experienced agents, versus 3–5% on internet-form aged.

Direct-mail aged FE leads run $8–25 per exclusive aged lead — significantly more per lead than internet-form. The math still often favors DM aged, because the close rate lift more than offsets the per-lead premium, particularly for agents with a strong phone close.

Not every vendor carries DM response inventory. If it matters to your operation, ask specifically.

## Compliance basics for life insurance leads

Aged internet-form life insurance leads carry the consumer's original TCPA consent from the time of the form fill. That consent is the legal basis for your outreach. Request the consent documentation — the actual disclosure language and timestamp — from your provider before building a campaign. Don't rely on a verbal assurance that consent exists.

Two active items for 2026:

The FCC's 1:1 consent rule for new lead-generation forms was vacated in January 2025 and fully repealed in August 2025. That doesn't retroactively affect aged inventory. Leads generated under prior consent frameworks are covered by the consent that existed at the time of the form fill.

Revocation is fully in force. A consumer's right to stop outreach via "any reasonable means" — text, phone, email, verbal — must be honored by your CRM in real time. If someone says stop, that stops the campaign the same day, not the next batch cycle.

For final expense campaigns: Florida's FTSA imposes tighter restrictions on text messaging to Florida residents, with private right of action and statutory damages per message. Oklahoma's OTSA, Washington's HB 1497, Maryland, and Texas have their own mini-TCPA frameworks. If you're running FE campaigns to FL numbers, use phone calls only.

## How to allocate your life insurance lead budget

For a multi-product agency with $3,000–$5,000 per month in lead spend:

- **40% final expense aged 15–85 day internet-form** — volume engine, lowest CPA, good entry point for multi-product cross-sell
- **25% aged IUL 15–85 day** — highest net per closing, lowest competition, demands a consultative phone close
- **20% aged term 15–85 day** — gateway product, cross-sell pipeline to FE or IUL conversion
- **15% real-time live transfer FE** — premium close-day inventory for experienced telesales agents who can convert on first contact

For a final-expense-only agent at $500–$1,500 per month:

- **70% aged internet-form FE 15–85 day** — the volume and CPA engine
- **20% aged direct-mail response FE** — higher per-lead cost, materially higher conversion for agents with a strong phone close
- **10% real-time or live transfer FE** — keep a small tap of premium inventory open for tested scripts

The scaling trigger worth tracking: cost per closing. Below $500 CPA on FE aged, or below $1,000 CPA on IUL aged, the math supports scaling. Above those thresholds, the diagnostic is almost always cadence, script, or filtering — not the leads themselves.

## What this means for you

Aged life insurance leads are the most economically diverse lead category in insurance sales. The sub-vertical breakdown matters: term is a volume play, FE is a telesales-and-scale play, IUL is a high-ceiling play that rewards patience and a consultative close.

A few things to run on Monday:

- Decide which sub-vertical to start with based on your commission structure, not your comfort zone. If you sell IUL, aged IUL at $2–6 per lead is the obvious first test.
- Order 250–500 leads in your primary vertical, filtered tightly to your best-performing states. Run a 14-day cadence before drawing conclusions.
- Track cost per closing from lead one. The agents who give up after 10 days are treating aged like real-time. Aged leads compound.
- Request TCPA consent documentation with your first order. Don't skip this step.
- If you're running FE campaigns to Florida numbers: phone calls only.
